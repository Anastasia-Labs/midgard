\section{Data availability layer}
\label{h:data-availability-layer}

The data availability layer is critical to Midgard's security because every committed block needs to be publicly available throughout the maturity period so that watchers can detect and prove fraud before invalid blocks are merged.

\subsection{Current committee-retained payload}
\label{h:data-availability-current-committee}

The current implementation uses a DA committee that retains exact
\code{DaPayloadEnvelopeV1} records keyed by \code{header\_hash}. Each envelope
declares identity or zstd encoding and authenticates the canonical
\code{DaPayloadV1} inner bytes, decoded length, and SHA-256.
The retained payload contains the embedded header, final UTxO members,
withdrawal members, forced and normal compact transaction sources, their full
canonical preimages, CEK program material, deposit members, transition trace
members, event-to-step members, validation-trace descriptors and retained step witnesses, and counts.
Committee nodes recompute every root and count against the L1 header before signing an attestation. The signature covers the versioned availability commitment derived from the exact retained envelope, deployment identity, header hash, bond-owner credential, and response geometry. It is not merely a signature of the header. Attestation application also creates the coupled availability bond. Queue merge accepts only the authenticated \code{Attested} or \code{Published} states; a challenge changes availability state and can block merging.
Final UTxO members carry canonical output-reference and full-output CBOR bytes.
For \code{utxos\_root}, committee nodes and challengers independently fold each
full output into its exact \code{LedgerOutputCommitmentV1} descriptor and use
that descriptor as the MPT value. The full bytes are retained rather than
replaced by the descriptor so all bounded output and reference-script openings
remain constructible.

Independent challengers retrieve the retained payload from DA committee routes keyed by deployment fingerprint and header hash, then reconstruct the roots and proof witnesses locally.
Operator debug endpoints are not a production DA source; challenger tooling must be able to build transition-trace proofs from the L1 header plus committee-retained data.

\begin{equation*}
    \T{DaPayloadV1} \coloneq \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{version} : & \T{Int} \\
        \T{block\_body} : & \T{DaPayloadBodyV1}
    \end{array} \right\}
\end{equation*}

\begingroup
\small
\begin{equation*}
    \T{DaPayloadBodyV1} \coloneq \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{header\_hash} : & \T{HeaderHash} \\
        \T{header} : & \T{Header} \\
        \T{utxos} : & \T{[(ByteArray, ByteArray)]} \\
        \T{withdrawals} : & \T{[(WithdrawalId, WithdrawalInfo)]} \\
        \T{forced\_transactions} : & \T{[(TxOrderId, ForcedInclusionTxV1)]} \\
        \T{transactions} : & \T{[(TxId, L2TransactionSourceV1)]} \\
        \T{transaction\_preimages} : & \T{[(TxId, ByteArray)]} \\
        \T{forced\_transaction\_preimages} : & \T{[(TxOrderId, ByteArray)]} \\
        \T{cek\_program\_material} : & \T{[(CekRoot, ByteArray)]} \\
        \T{deposits} : & \T{[(DepositId, DepositInfo)]} \\
        \T{transition\_trace} : & \T{[(Int, TransitionStep)]} \\
        \T{event\_to\_step} : & \T{[(EventKey, EventToStepValue)]} \\
        \T{validation\_traces} : & \T{[(EventKey, ValidationTraceDescriptorV1)]} \\
        \T{validation\_trace\_witnesses} : & \T{[(ByteArray, ByteArray)]} \\
        \T{counts} : & \T{HeaderCounts}
    \end{array} \right\}
\end{equation*}
\endgroup

The \code{transactions} member is the exact
\code{Map(TxId, L2TransactionSourceV1)} projection committed by
\code{transactions\_root}.  The \code{forced\_transactions} member is the
exact \code{Map(TxOrderId, ForcedInclusionTxV1)} projection committed by
\code{forced\_transactions\_root}; its key is the serialized L1 output
reference, even when several orders carry the same L2 transaction.  The
corresponding preimage arrays use those same keys and contain the canonical
native transaction bytes.  The machine-readable contract and cross-language
golden are generated from
\code{demo/midgard-node/tests/fixtures/transaction-root-v1.canonical.json}.

\subsection{Alternative DA integrations}
\label{h:data-availability-leios}
\label{h:data-availability-multisig}

Leios blob storage and a stake-weighted Mithril integration remain possible design directions, not capabilities of the current committee implementation. The durable requirement is independently retrievable, authenticated block and proof material throughout the challenge and correction lifecycle. Any replacement must define onchain authentication, retention, retrieval, failure recovery, and a sound relationship to the block commitments before replacing the committee protocol. A signature alone does not guarantee future retrieval, and equal participating stake does not establish equivalence between the security properties of different protocols.
