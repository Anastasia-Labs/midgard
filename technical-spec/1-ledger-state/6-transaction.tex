\section{Transaction}
\label{h:transaction}

A canonical V1 transaction source set is a finite map from transaction ID to the
authenticated L2 transaction proof source.  The ID is the Blake2b-256 hash of
the compact transaction body; equivalently, the map key must match the
\code{tx\_id} field in the source value.  The witness-set hash, field lengths,
and transaction commitment are authenticated by the block's
\code{transactions\_root} through that value, but they are not part of the
transaction ID:
\begin{align*}
    \T{TxSetV1} &\coloneq \T{Map(TxId, L2TransactionSourceV1)} \\
      &\coloneq \Bigl\{
        (k_i: \T{TxId}, v_i: \T{L2TransactionSourceV1}) \mid
        k_i \equiv v_i.\T{tx\_id} ,\;
        \forall i \neq j.\; k_i \neq k_j
    \Bigr\}
\end{align*}

The exact source value is:
\begin{align*}
    \T{L2TransactionSourceV1} \coloneq \left\{
      \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{tx\_id} : & \T{TxId} \\
        \T{transaction\_commitment} : & \T{MidgardTxProofCommitmentV1} \\
        \T{source} : & \T{NativeTxProofSourceV1}
      \end{array} \right\} \\
    \T{NativeTxProofSourceV1} \coloneq \left\{
      \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{compact\_cbor} : & \T{ByteArray} \\
        \T{witness\_set\_compact\_cbor} : & \T{ByteArray} \\
        \T{field\_preimage\_lengths\_cbor} : & \T{ByteArray}
      \end{array} \right\}
\end{align*}

\T{MidgardTxProofCommitmentV1} is the domain-separated hash
\(\mathcal{H}_{\T{Blake2b-256}}(\T{"MidgardNativeTxProofSourceV1"}
\mathbin{\Vert} \T{Cbor(1)} \mathbin{\Vert}
\T{Cbor(source)})\), matching the production V1 encoder.

The \code{transactions\_root} is the source root for normal L2 transaction requests.
Forced L1 transaction orders are committed separately in \code{forced\_transactions\_root}, keyed by their L1 order identity.
The normal root uses the \code{TransactionsV1RootDomain} counted-root domain,
while the forced root uses the distinct
\code{ForcedTransactionsV1RootDomain} domain.
Only accepted normal L2 transaction requests may appear in \code{transactions\_root}.
Invalid normal L2 transaction requests are excluded before commitment; they do not become no-op source events in the transition trace.
If two accepted normal L2 transactions share the same \code{TxId}, the block body is not canonical because \code{transactions\_root} is keyed by \code{TxId}; production block construction fails closed rather than choosing one silently.

An L2 transaction request in a Midgard block is an endogenous event.
Its corresponding utxo set transition is validated purely based on the information contained in the utxo set.
This contrasts with deposit and withdrawal events, which create and spend utxos (respectively) based on information observed outside the L2 ledger.

\paragraph{Canonical V1 consensus profile.}
The transaction structures below describe the canonical protocol-version (1),
native-transaction-version (1) surface. Canonical V1 enables public-key,
native-script, PlutusV3, and MidgardV1 authorization together with mint and
burn, reference inputs, script witnesses, redeemers, observers, reference
scripts, script payment credentials, and protected outputs. These features are
part of the normative V1 profile rather than dormant codec fields. Unknown
versions, languages, purposes, or encodings fail closed.

Canonical V1 has no independent (8{,}192)-byte whole-transaction ceiling.
Each canonical transaction aggregate preimage is bounded independently at
(32{,}768) bytes and is authenticated and revealed in chunks of at most
(4{,}095) bytes. A ledger-output preimage is at most (16{,}384) bytes, and
the canonical CBOR encoding of an output value is at most (5{,}000) bytes.
Reference scripts are supported: a consensus program envelope is at most
(50) bytes and commits to at most (1{,}597{,}819) canonical CEK material
nodes and (67{,}108{,}418) structural material bytes, with blob chunks of at
most (4{,}095) bytes. Wrapping all independently bounded transaction
preimages in the canonical encoding gives the exact derived full-transaction
maximum of (295{,}041) bytes. None of these limits is operator-tunable policy.

Script-controlled spends and mint or burn are admitted only when the
corresponding scripts and policies authorize them. Their effects participate
in the mint-aware value-preservation equation in
\cref{h:custom-midgard-ledger-rules}.

\subsection{Cardano transaction types}
\label{h:cardano-transaction-types}

Cardano's L1 transaction type (\href{https://github.com/IntersectMBO/cardano-ledger/blob/cardano-ledger-conway-1.17.2.0/eras/conway/impl/src/Cardano/Ledger/Conway/Tx.hs}{Chang 2 hardfork}) served as an initial model for Midgard's L2 transaction type.
In the following, field types prefixed by a question mark \code{?} are set to appropriate ``empty'' defaults during deserialization if the serialized transaction omits them:\footnote{In this section, we are mainly concerned with comparing Cardano and Midgard's deserialized data types.
Serialization formats and conversions are addressed in Midgard's CDDL specifications.} \footnote{For simplicity of exposition, we omit the ``era'' type parameters, effectively coercing them all to the Conway era.
We also simplify the script types.}
\begingroup
\allowdisplaybreaks
\begin{align*}
    \T{CardanoTx} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{body} : & \T{CardanoTxBody} \\
        \T{wits} : & \T{CardanoTxWits} \\
        \T{is\_valid} : & \T{Bool} \\
        \T{auxiliary\_data} : & \quad?\;\T{TxMetadata}
    \end{array} \right\} \\
    \T{CardanoTxBody} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{spend\_inputs} : & \T{Set(OutputRef)} \\
        \T{collateral\_inputs} : & \quad?\;\T{Set(OutputRef)} \\
        \T{reference\_inputs} : & \quad?\;\T{Set(OutputRef)} \\
        \T{outputs} : & \T{[Output]} \\
        \T{collateral\_return} : & \quad?\;\T{Output} \\
        \T{total\_collateral} : & \quad?\;\T{Coin} \\
        \T{certificates} : & \quad?\;\T{[ Set(Certificate) ]} \\
        \T{withdrawals} : & \quad?\;\T{Map(RewardAccount, Coin)} \\
        \T{fee} : & \T{Coin} \\
        \T{validity\_interval} : & \T{CardanoValidityInterval} \\
        \T{required\_signer\_hashes} : & \quad?\;\T{Set(VKeyHash)} \\
        \T{mint} : & \quad?\;\T{Value} \\
        \T{script\_integrity\_hash} : & \quad?\;\T{Hash} \\
        \T{auxiliary\_data\_hash} : & \quad?\;\T{Hash} \\
        \T{network\_id} : & \quad?\;\T{Network} \\
        \T{voting\_procedures} : & \quad?\;\T{VotingProcedures} \\
        \T{proposal\_procedures} : & \quad?\;\T{Set(ProposalProcedure)} \\
        \T{current\_treasury\_value} : & \quad?\;\T{Coin} \\
        \T{treasury\_donation} : & \quad?\;\T{Coin}
    \end{array} \right\}\\
    \T{CardanoTxWits} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{addr\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\T{Set(VKey, Signature)} \\
        \T{boot\_addr\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\T{Set(BootstrapWitness)} \\
        \T{script\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\T{Map(ScriptHash, CardanoScript)} \\
        \T{data\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\T{TxDats} \\
        \T{redeemer\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\T{Redeemers}
    \end{array} \right\}\\
    \T{CardanoValidityInterval} \coloneq\;& (\T{Option(Slot), Option(Slot)}) \\ 
    \T{CardanoScript} \coloneq\;& \T{TimelockScript}(\T{Timelock}) \\
                          \mid\;& \T{PlutusScript}(\T{CardanoPlutusVersion},\T{PlutusBinary}) \\
    \T{CardanoPlutusVersion} \coloneq\;& \T{PlutusV1} \\
                                 \mid\;& \T{PlutusV2} \\
                                 \mid\;& \T{PlutusV3}
\end{align*}
\endgroup

\subsection{Deviations from the Cardano transaction types}
\label{h:deviations-from-cardano-transaction-types}

Midgard's transaction types deviate from Cardano's in the following ways:
\begin{description}
    \item[No staking or governance actions.] Midgard's consensus protocol is not based on Ouroboros proof-of-stake, and its governance protocol is not based on Cardano's hard-fork-combinator update mechanism.
      Furthermore, Midgard's L1 scripts cannot authorize arbitrary staking or governance actions on behalf of users.
      For this reason, staking and governance actions are prohibited in Midgard L2 transactions.
    \item[No pre-Conway features.] Midgard does not need to maintain backwards compatibility with pre-Conway eras.
      Midgard deposits and transactions will be prohibited from using bootstrap addresses, and Shelley addresses with Plutus versions older than Plutus V3.
      Canonical V1 admits public-key and native-script credentials together
      with PlutusV3 and MidgardV1 programs and script payment credentials;
      unknown or pre-Conway language versions fail closed.
    \item[No transaction metadata.] Midgard prohibits metadata in L2
      transactions. Canonical V1 therefore requires
      \code{auxiliary\_data\_hash} to equal the empty-null root; a non-empty
      hash fails closed because no authenticated auxiliary-data preimage is
      carried by the transaction.
    \item[No datum hashes in outputs.] Midgard output datums are either absent or inline.
      Datum-hash outputs are invalid in Midgard L2 transactions and deposits, and transactions do not carry a datum-witness bucket.
      This avoids maintaining a datum-hash to datum witness map and makes each UTxO's script-visible datum fully determined by the UTxO itself.
    \item[CIPs 112 and 128.] Midgard has adopted \href{https://github.com/cardano-foundation/CIPs/tree/master/CIP-0112}{CIP-112} and \href{https://github.com/cardano-foundation/CIPs/tree/master/CIP-0128}{CIP 128} ahead of Cardano mainnet, which we expect to merge them in 2025.
      CIP-112 provides a new ``Observe'' script purpose, a more principled and streamlined alternative to the ``Withdraw 0'' trick widely used by most Cardano dApps.
      It also allows native scripts to conditionally forward their logic to an observer script.
      On the other hand, CIP-128 will significantly improve the execution efficiency achievable in Plutus contracts by preserving the order of inputs in a submitted transaction (instead of ordering them by \code{OutputRef}).
    \item[Different network ID.] Midgard transactions and utxo addresses use a different network ID to distinguish them from their Cardano mainnet counterparts.
      Furthermore, we are considering a prefix to minting policy IDs to distinguish tokens deposited from L1 from tokens minted on L2.
    \item[Replace slots with POSIX timestamps.] Midgard's consensus protocol does not use slots like Ouroboros.
      Furthermore, the L1 contracts that enforce Midgard's consensus protocol are evaluated in the Plutus script context, where slots are converted to Posix timestamps.
      For these reasons, Midgard uses POSIX timestamps instead of slots in L2 transactions.
    \item[IsValid tag is always True.] Midgard's consensus protocol does not use the IsValid tag, so it is always set to \code{True}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Midgard transaction types}
\label{h:midgard-transaction-types}

Midgard's transaction types modify the above simplified types by replacing all variable-length fields with hashes (indicated by the letter $\mathcal{H}$ below).
The data availability layer is responsible for confirming that the hashes correspond to their preimages, and DA fraud proofs can be submitted if this correspondence is violated.

\begingroup
\allowdisplaybreaks
\begin{align*}
    \T{TxId} \coloneq\;& \mathcal{H}_\T{Blake2b-256}(\T{MidgardTxBody}) \\
    \T{MidgardTxCompactWithoutValidity} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{body} : & \T{TxId} \\
        \T{wits} : & \mathcal{H}(\T{MidgardTxWits})
    \end{array} \right\} \\
    \T{MidgardTxCompact} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{body} : & \T{TxId} \\
        \T{wits} : & \mathcal{H}(\T{MidgardTxWits}) \\
        \T{validity} : & \T{MidgardTxValidity} \\
    \end{array} \right\} \\
    \T{MidgardTxBody} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{spend\_inputs} : & \mathcal{H}(\T{[OutputRef]}) \\
        \T{reference\_inputs} : & \quad?\;\mathcal{H}(\T{[OutputRef]}) \\
        \T{outputs} : & \mathcal{H}(\T{[Output]}) \\
        \T{fee} : & \T{Coin} \\
        \T{validity\_interval} : & \quad?\;\T{MidgardSValidityInterval} \\
        \T{required\_observers} : & \quad?\;\mathcal{H}(\T{[ScriptCredential]}) \\
        \T{required\_signer\_hashes} : & \quad?\;\mathcal{H}(\T{[VKeyCredential]}) \\
        \T{mint} : & \quad?\;\T{Value} \\
        \T{script\_integrity\_hash} : & \quad?\;\T{Hash} \\
        \T{auxiliary\_data\_hash} : & \quad?\;\T{Hash} \\
        \T{network\_id} : & \quad?\;\T{Network}
    \end{array} \right\}\\
    \T{MidgardTxWits} \coloneq\;& \left\{
    \begin{array}{ll}
        \T{addr\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\mathcal{H}(\T{Set(VKey, Signature)}) \\
        \T{script\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\mathcal{H}(\T{Map(ScriptHash, MidgardSScript)}) \\
        \T{redeemer\_tx\_wits} : & \quad?\;\mathcal{H}(\T{Redeemers})
    \end{array} \right\}\\
    \T{MidgardTxValidity} \coloneq\;& \T{TxIsValid} \\
                              \mid\;& \T{NonExistentInputUtxo} \\
                              \mid\;& \T{InvalidSignature} \\
                              \mid\;& \T{FailedScript} \\
                              \mid\;& \T{FeeTooLow} \\
                              \mid\;& \T{UnbalancedTx} \\
                              \mid\;& \T{etc.}
\end{align*}
\endgroup

\subsection{Midgard-native wire format (v1)}
\label{h:midgard-native-transaction-wire-format}

Midgard's canonical transaction wire format is \emph{not} Cardano's transaction type.
Instead, Midgard serializes transactions as versioned, fixed-order tuples designed for fast decoding and fraud-proof construction.
Ledger validation accepts only this Midgard-native format; Cardano transaction payloads are rejected.

The compact form is the commitment committed in blocks:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
  \T{MidgardTxCompactV1} \coloneq (&\T{version}=1,\; \T{body\_hash},\; \T{wits\_hash},\; \T{validity\_code}) \\
  \T{body\_hash} \coloneq&\; \mathcal{H}_{\text{Blake2b-256}}(\T{MidgardTxBodyCompactV1}) \\
  \T{wits\_hash} \coloneq&\; \mathcal{H}_{\text{Blake2b-256}}(\T{MidgardTxWitsCompactV1})
\end{split}
\end{equation*}

The body compact contains only fixed-size commitments and scalar fields:
\begin{equation*}
\small
\begin{split}
\T{MidgardTxBodyCompactV1} \coloneq (&
\T{spend\_inputs\_root},\;
\T{reference\_inputs\_root},\;
\T{outputs\_root},\;
\T{fee},\\
&\T{valid\_from},\;
\T{valid\_to},\;
\T{required\_observers\_root},\;
\T{required\_signers\_root},\\
&\T{mint\_root},\;
\T{script\_integrity\_hash},\;
\T{auxiliary\_data\_hash},\;
\T{network\_id})
\end{split}
\end{equation*}

where \code{valid\_from} and \code{valid\_to} use \code{-1} as the sentinel for an unbounded side of the validity interval.

The full form reveals preimages needed for offchain checks and proof generation while preserving the same compact commitments:
\begin{equation*}
\small
\begin{split}
\T{MidgardTxBodyFullV1} \coloneq (&
\T{spend\_inputs\_root},\;
\T{spend\_inputs\_preimage},\;
\T{reference\_inputs\_root},\;
\T{reference\_inputs\_preimage},\\
&\T{outputs\_root},\;
\T{outputs\_preimage},\;
\T{fee},\;
\T{valid\_from},\;
\T{valid\_to},\\
&\T{required\_observers\_root},\;
\T{required\_observers\_preimage},\;
\T{required\_signers\_root},\\
&\T{required\_signers\_preimage},\;
\T{mint\_root},\;
\T{mint\_preimage},\;
\T{script\_integrity\_hash},\\
&\T{auxiliary\_data\_hash},\;
\T{network\_id})
\end{split}
\end{equation*}

and analogously for witnesses with three \code{(root, preimage)} pairs:
\code{addr\_tx\_wits}, \code{script\_tx\_wits}, and \code{redeemer\_tx\_wits}.

This layout keeps fraud-proof-critical roots directly accessible, avoids map-key scanning during decoding, and cleanly separates block commitments (compact) from data-availability payloads (full).
