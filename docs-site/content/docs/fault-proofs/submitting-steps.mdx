---
title: "Submitting Proof Steps"
description: "The multi-step fraud-proof submission flow driven by the fault-proof tooling."
---

A fraud proof is verified on-chain across several transactions by the
[computation-thread state machine](/docs/onchain/fraud-proof-machines). The
fault-proof tooling drives this step by step.

## Before you start

Each submit command needs a prepared proof artifact, a live challenge target, and an
L1 connection:

- The JSON artifacts written by a [prepare command](/docs/fault-proofs/preparing-proofs).
  For commands that expose `compatibility.canUseSubmitStepCommands`, it must be
  `true`; strict preparers instead abort before writing artifacts when their
  supplied block commitment does not match.
- `--blueprint <path>` and `--deployment-info <path>`. Run `inspect-contracts` first
  to confirm the two agree.
- A funded prover wallet and a provider (`--provider Blockfrost` or `Kupmios`).
- A block still inside its challenge window. A merged block cannot be challenged.

## Flow

<Steps>
  <Step>
    **Init**, `submit-init` mints the **computation-thread** token, which opens
    the challenge and tracks it.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Step through**, `submit-step-01` … `submit-step-04` advance the
    computation thread one step at a time, each verifying one part of the proof
    on-chain.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Conclude**, the final step mints the permanent `fraud_proof` token and
    burns the computation thread. The token is the record of a concluded proof,
    not a claim in progress.
  </Step>
  <Step>
    **Category variants**, some categories have their own step sequence:
    `submit-invalid-range-step-01` and `-02` for the `invalid-range` category,
    `submit-non-existent-input-step-01` through `-04` for the
    `non-existent-input` category, and `submit-no-reference-input-step-01`
    through `-04` for the `no-reference-input` category, and
    `submit-input-no-idx-step-01` through `-04` for the `input-no-idx` category.
    The non-existent-input steps consume the `ne-*.json` artifacts in order: tx
    inclusion, inputs preimage, ledger non-membership, then txs non-membership on
    the concluding step. The no-reference-input steps mirror that order with the
    `nri-*.json` artifacts, taking `--reference-inputs-preimage` and
    `--bad-reference-input-index` on step 02. The input-no-idx steps take the
    `input-no-idx-*.json` artifacts and need no ledger proofs: bad-tx inclusion,
    inputs preimage, then the producing transaction's inclusion on step 03 and its
    `--outputs-preimage` on the concluding step.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warn" title="The two tokens are not the same">
  `submit-init` mints the computation-thread token. The `fraud_proof` token is
  minted only on the final step, once the proof has concluded. A computation
  thread in flight proves nothing.
</Callout>

The `transition-trace/` module supports the transition-trace proof used to
demonstrate how state evolved between blocks.

## What success and failure look like

Each command writes its result to stdout as JSON. A successful step returns the
submitted transaction hash and the advanced thread state. A step fails when the
deployment does not match the blueprint, the referenced thread has already advanced
past that step, or the on-chain validator rejects the supplied witness. A rejected
step leaves the computation thread where it was; re-run the same step with corrected
material rather than skipping ahead.

Once a `fraud_proof` token exists, continue to
[removing the block](/docs/fault-proofs/removing-blocks).

<Callout type="info" title="Manual tooling">
  This is manual operator tooling (`bin: midgard-fault-proofs`) intended for
  producing and driving proofs.
</Callout>
