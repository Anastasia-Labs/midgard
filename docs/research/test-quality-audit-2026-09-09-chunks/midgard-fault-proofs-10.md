- **Template family 1 - "publication fit" (14 files).** All build the real applied validators from the real blueprint, publish them as reference-script UTxOs on a real Lucid emulator, and assert the signed transaction is inside the external Cardano max-tx-size (16384) or the repo's 512-byte reliability reserve (15872). The limit is a genuine external contract and the value is measured, not transcribed, so these are legitimate. The systematic defect is the gate: 9 of them wrap the suite in `describe.runIf(hasFamily)` over the blueprint titles, which fails OPEN - a renamed or dropped validator family makes the whole suite silently disappear and CI stays green (testing-best-practices section 14). Three siblings (transaction-output-non-canonical, spend-input-signer-missing, output-reference-script-decoding, redeemer-canonicity) instead assert the titles exist and are the correct template. `script-integrity-hash-mismatch` additionally hard-pins the five compiled sizes.
- **Template family 2 - van-Rossem fit ledgers (10 files).** Every one of these reads a JSON size plan from `docs/fault-proofs/size-plans/`, derives the blueprint sha256 from the REAL blueprint at test time and compares, then re-runs `buildVanRossemFitLedger` over the stored rows and compares to the stored ledger. Unlike the self-comparing family the earlier audit found, none of these compare the digest to a copy of itself - the digest oracle is independent and correct. The remaining weakness is different: the measurement rows themselves are transcribed numbers, not executions (section 13 - "a saved measurement is historical evidence, not a new execution"), and several files pin a literal compiler version and hand-maintained entry/shape counts. `min-fee`, `invalid-signature`, `native-script-invalid` and `value-and-mint` derive compilerVersion from `blueprint.preamble.compiler.version` and are the correct template; `cek-*`, `no-reference-input`, `non-existent-input`, `native-script-decoding-workflow` and `value-not-preserved` hardcode `aiken v1.1.23+5adf783`.
- **Circular provenance between the two ledger halves.** The ledgers that `*-fit-ledger.test.ts` verifies are WRITTEN by the sibling lifecycle tests in this same chunk: `non-existent-input-wrongful-rejection-lifecycle.test.ts` (L65-L89), `no-reference-input-wrongful-rejection-lifecycle.test.ts` (L65-L89), `min-fee-wrongful-rejection-lifecycle.test.ts` (L67-L87) and `transition-trace-installed-lifecycle.test.ts` (L127-L153) all call `writeVanRossemFitLedger` into `docs/fault-proofs/size-plans/`. So a fit-ledger test is a baseline copied from current output (section 12): it freezes approved behavior and detects tampering/staleness, but it cannot independently prove the size plan is satisfied. The lifecycle tests DO assert the real envelope bounds on every measured transaction, which is where the actual protection lives. The three lifecycle writers gate their ledger write - and, unfortunately, the completeness assertion that a required scenario ran - behind `MIDGARD_WRITE_FIT_LEDGER=1`, so in CI those completeness guards never execute; `transition-trace-installed-lifecycle` is the exception and asserts its five named cases completed unconditionally.
