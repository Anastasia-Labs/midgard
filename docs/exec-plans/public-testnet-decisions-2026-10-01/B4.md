# B4 — recover provable state, keep unresolved integrity faults stopped

**Recommendation:** approve automatic recovery when the component can prove the correct state from authenticated Cardano history and its preserved local records. Do not approve a blanket rule that clears every quarantine after a retry or restart. Keep an explicit **needs human intervention** outcome for faults the software cannot safely resolve. Keep `/healthz` as process liveness, and use `/readyz` plus a durable incident status to make recovery and intervention visible.

This is a decision recommendation, not an implemented fix. It describes the source read on 2026-10-01. D3 separately covers the committee-wide cost and the obligation to answer challenges for previously signed payloads; A5 separately decides the availability journal's refusal scope.

**What “quarantine” means in ordinary language.** A committee member or watcher has stopped trusting some information it previously used. It refuses work that could depend on that information. This can be the right safety response: if a saved transaction was considered final but the chain now contradicts it, blindly carrying on can reuse money incorrectly, sign on the wrong history, or lose track of an outstanding challenge. The problem is when the stop has no safe route out even after the correct history becomes available.

“Re-derive” means reconstructing the affected local conclusions from verified chain history, checking their dependencies, and recording why the replacement is valid. It does not mean deleting the error and trying the same operation again. A valid recovery changes the evidence supporting the conclusion; a restart changes only the process.

**The current behavior is more selective than the earlier brief suggests.** Ordinary observation failures already avoid durable committee quarantine. The source explicitly separates timeouts, reads at different chain points, a chain moving during a read, and history not yet final from contradictions at a proven point or corrupt saved history. The former fail the current tick and retry; only the designated integrity error writes quarantine. [Committee failure classification](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/l1/source-integrity.ts#L1), lines 1–30; [actual quarantine gate](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L930), lines 930–948.

The committee's integrity quarantine really is terminal today: the normal state merge rejects a return from `quarantined` to `healthy`, and the next tick returns immediately without scanning Cardano again. Rebooting with the same database therefore cannot cure it. Configuration or network/source-authority changes can also cause quarantine during startup, followed by a thrown error. [Terminal transition](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/store.persisted-decision-transition.ts#L11), lines 11–40; [tick refusal](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L561), lines 561–565; [startup identity check](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L191), lines 191–204.

The source contains concrete reasons for this stop: a previously decided header disappears, changes without verified intervening history, or loses finality; a rollback crosses a persisted decision; the durable chain cursor disagrees with the authority cursor; or the rollback journal has a gap. These are evidence problems, not interchangeable with an unavailable provider. [Decision checks](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.check-l1-rollback-feed.ts#L38), lines 38–68; [cursor and rollback checks](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.check-l1-rollback-feed.ts#L105), lines 105–157.

**The watcher already demonstrates the right kind of recovery.** Its live coordinator and restart path attempt authenticated recovery after a finality incident. Recovery verifies both the old and replacement histories, their exact common ancestor, their connection to the recorded incident, and the recovery-depth limit. It removes dependent orphan records together and preserves replacement evidence for replay. Incomplete or invalid evidence leaves quarantine in place. Consequently, B4 is not a request to invent automatic recovery everywhere from scratch; it is to extend this proven-in-source design discipline to the remaining terminal paths. The design exists in code; this report does not claim its runtime behavior was tested. live recovery attempt, chain-coordinator.create-coordinator.ts lines 481–508 (since deleted); restart recovery, chain-coordinator.canonical-path-from-history.ts lines 177–221 (since deleted); [recovery verifier](../../../demo/midgard-watcher/src/l1/rollback-engine/recovery.evaluate-watcher-post-finality-recovery-internal.ts#L45), lines 45–49 and 147–213.

**Cases and the recommended result:**

| Situation | Recommended behavior | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Provider timeout, disconnection, or incomplete catch-up | Wait and retry with bounded work; temporarily refuse operations needing fresh evidence. | No contradiction has been established. An unavailable answer does not invalidate saved decisions. |
| Ordinary rollback before the configured finality boundary | Rewind affected derived state and replay authenticated replacement history. | The old observation was provisional; the replacement can be explained and verified. |
| Rollback crossing a decision already treated as final | Enter recovery, freeze affected dependencies, and prove the old and new paths before resuming. | This is an incident. It can be repairable, but the mere appearance of a newer tip is insufficient. |
| One saved operation is contradicted, while shared chain authority remains trustworthy | Recover that operation and its dependents; allow work proven independent. | One affected header need not stop unrelated headers, but they may share wallet inputs or transaction ancestry. |
| Saved local record is corrupt, replay has unexplained gaps, or two authenticated surfaces disagree at the same chain point | Hold the affected scope; require human intervention unless a defined, verifiable repair can reconstruct everything required. | Repeatedly reading the same bad evidence cannot decide which facts are safe. |
| Wrong network, deployment, committee membership, or source-authority identity | Refuse dependent work and require an explicit configuration/migration decision. | Software must not automatically decide which deployment or authority the operator intended. |
| Recovery history has been pruned, exceeds the supported depth, or is missing | Remain stopped, state exactly what evidence is missing, and escalate. Resume only after a supported authenticated recovery becomes possible. | “We cannot prove it” must not become “probably safe.” |
| Two different payload envelopes were supplied for one header | Retain conflict evidence and stop the affected signing decision. Recover only if the protocol establishes the correct commitment and exact bytes unambiguously. | A successful fetch or internally valid envelope alone does not resolve conflicting commitments. |

The watcher currently caps supported post-finality recovery at **2,160 blocks**, with the verifier also enforcing the configured maximum. This is a source constant, not a measured guarantee that every incident within that distance is recoverable: the necessary history and bindings still have to exist. [Depth constant](../../../demo/midgard-watcher/src/l1/rollback-engine/types.ts#L44), lines 44–48; [depth enforcement](../../../demo/midgard-watcher/src/l1/rollback-engine/recovery.evaluate-watcher-post-finality-recovery-internal.ts#L204), lines 204–213.

**How recovery should work.** Record the incident durably, including its reason, original chain point, affected decisions, deployment identity, and the last trusted anchor. Suspend new signing and spending that depend on it. A dedicated recovery operation must remain available even while ordinary mutation is refused; otherwise the guard also blocks its own repair. Fetch authenticated history, verify its continuity, reconstruct the affected decisions and resource reservations, and atomically publish the repaired state with the evidence explaining it. Recheck the incident generation and chain identity before publishing, so an old recovery attempt cannot overwrite a newer rollback.

Keep original signed bytes and recorded commitments immutable. Recovery may conclude that a transaction is still included, has returned to pending, safely expired, or conflicts with a replacement spend. It must not rewrite the transaction's identity or assume it never existed. Do not submit a new competing transaction until canonical inclusion, validity expiry, and the input dependencies justify it.

This matters particularly for the availability journal. It saves signed bytes, inputs, collateral, expected outputs, and expiry; it records transaction dependencies and reserves resources. Normal confirmation releases reservations, and normal transitions refuse already-confirmed records. Therefore recovering a confirmation that disappeared is a dedicated reconciliation operation across descendants and wallet resources, not an ordinary status toggle. The journal's current halt is a persistent metadata flag with no clearing method in its public interface. Watcher chain recovery alone does not clear that flag. [Intent contents](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/availability-operation-journal.ts#L5), lines 5–24; [dependencies](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/availability-operation-journal.ts#L529), lines 529–535; [transition and release rules](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/availability-operation-journal.ts#L546), lines 546–578; [halt interface and persistence](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/availability-operation-journal.ts#L121), lines 121–123 and 594–599.

**Scope follows dependency, not the word “quarantine.”** A bad payload can usually hold its header and dependent actions. A contradicted signed transaction can also affect later transactions spending its outputs, or a shared wallet whose inputs were released after confirmation. Those resources must be frozen and reconstructed before unrelated spending is admitted. A corrupt common chain journal or wrong deployment identity affects every decision relying on that authority, so a global hold is appropriate. Independent work may continue only when independence is established. A5 decides the journal's default blast radius; B4 must preserve this dependency rule whichever API is chosen.

**Bound retries without abandoning eventual recovery.** Each fetch and replay batch needs an explicit deadline and limits on concurrency, bytes, and history processed. Use capped backoff for transport failures and persist the recovery cursor, so restarts resume useful work. After a bounded burst makes no progress, enter a waiting state instead of repeatedly replaying the same evidence; retry on meaningful events such as provider reconnection, new authenticated chain history, or newly available payload bytes. A timeout can pause a recovery attempt and raise an alert. It cannot prove a contradiction resolved. Repeated deterministic validation failures should move to the human-required outcome, preserving evidence. Retry exhaustion alone should never authorize deletion, signing, input release, or database reset.

The retry timings are implementation settings that need load and outage testing, not a protocol ruling made by this note. The important boundaries are bounded resource use, persistent progress, no busy loop on unchanged bad evidence, and proof-gated resumption. Recovery retention must also cover the supported rewind depth; otherwise the program eventually loses its own ability to repair. A4 and E3 handle that retention and catch-up policy.

**What the health endpoints should say.** Today the committee's `/healthz` always returns 200 when its server handles the request; `/readyz` returns 503 for an unready snapshot, including quarantine. The node's handler likewise explicitly defines `/healthz` as server liveness. [Committee endpoints](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/api/server.ts#L82), lines 82–89; [committee readiness](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L420), lines 420–426 and 462–472; [node liveness handler](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/commands/listen-router.post-tx-status-batch-handler.ts#L203), lines 203–215.

I recommend preserving that distinction. During recovery, return liveness success while the process and recovery owner remain responsive, and readiness failure for the affected role. For a human-required fault, remain unready and publish `intervention_required`, the stable incident identifier, affected scope, missing/contradictory evidence, last progress, and next permitted recovery action. Alert on entering this state. `/healthz` should fail for a dead or irreparably wedged process where replacing the process can help, rather than for a durable protocol refusal that survives replacement.

The benefit is avoiding an endless restart cycle that consumes resources and can interrupt retained-data service without repairing the database. The tradeoff is that a simple “HTTP 200 means all is well” monitor is insufficient. Monitoring must check readiness and incident status, and recovery inactivity must be observable. If the owner wants a single overall-status endpoint, it can report failure for intervention-required incidents, but it must not be wired as an unconditional restart trigger. This answers B4's second question: **remaining terminal integrity cases should fail readiness and alert; they should not automatically fail process liveness.**

Current committee behavior has an additional relevant limit: lack of fresh authenticated L1 progress eventually invokes a bounded shutdown and a dedicated unavailable-view exit. Changing `/healthz` alone does not change that exit policy, nor does restarting make terminal saved quarantine healthy. The recovery implementation must integrate its progress and intervention states with that supervisor behavior. [L1 deadline and exit](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/tick-runner.ts#L104), lines 104–113 and 160–177. D2 separately addresses supervisor reliability.

**Alternatives and tradeoffs.** Leaving all integrity stops terminal is simpler and safest where no recovery proof exists, but needlessly prolongs incidents that verified replay could repair. Automatically clearing every stop maximizes apparent uptime while risking repeated signatures, conflicting spends, or acceptance of corrupt history; reject it. Proof-based recovery takes more implementation and verification effort, but preserves the safety reason for the original refusal and restores service once that reason is demonstrably resolved. That is the recommended balance for public testnet and production.

**Acceptance needed before claiming this is solved:** tests must show transient failures never create durable quarantine; incomplete or forged recovery evidence never clears it; a valid bounded rollback repairs every dependent record and reservation; unrelated work proceeds only with proven independence; crashes at each publication boundary resume without duplicate spending/signing; configuration mismatch remains human-required; and readiness accurately reports waiting, recovery, and intervention. A live outage/reorg drill must establish actual resumption on deployed artifacts. These are proposed acceptance criteria, not checks run for this report.
