# D1 — conflicting payloads: validate candidates, preserve signed promises

Recommendation, 2026-10-01: replace the single, permanently conflicting payload record with a bounded candidate store and a separate immutable record of the exact bytes promised by a committee signature. Reject bad candidates without letting them disable good data. Keep a refusal for genuine ambiguity between authenticated claims. Recover a refusal only when authoritative evidence establishes which exact commitment governs; a timeout or another successful download is not that evidence.

This is a better balance than either “first bytes win” or “every difference stops the header.” It requires a storage and signing change, coordinated with A8's producer authorization policy and B4's recovery design. Until that change is complete, retain the existing signing refusal for an unresolved conflict. Do not loosen it with a one-line first-arrival rule.

## What the problem is today

A payload contains the block data that watchers need to examine a block. A committee member eventually signs a promise to retain specific bytes. Before signing, the current upload endpoint accepts bytes after checking their size, hash and outer packaging. It does not check that their contents match the Cardano header at this stage. The resulting stored status is `fetched`, meaning received but unverified. [Upload checks](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/da/libp2p/payload-protocols.da-libp2p-payload-protocol-handlers.ts#L399).

If another payload with a different stored hash arrives for the same header, the save operation marks the record `conflicted`. This happens whether the existing record is fetched, rejected or verified. It builds the replacement record from the incoming payload, so this operation also does not preserve both full variants in the header's payload row. Both storage implementations use this resolver. [Conflict resolver](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/store.json-file-committee-store.ts#L818), [Postgres save](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/store/postgres.postgres-committee-store.ts#L536).

The committee refuses to verify a conflicted stored payload. Its peer-fetch path also groups candidates by hash and marks a conflict before checking their contents against the header. Consequently, an admitted hostile peer can submit a correctly packaged but unrelated payload and prevent the honest one from being used. This is an inferred attack from the source, not a live exploit demonstrated here. It is not an unrestricted internet upload: the private transport has peer admission, while A8 documents the missing role and header-specific authorization checks. [Stored refusal](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L1274), [candidate selection](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L1322).

The later verifier already checks the embedded header, its hash, the header observed on Cardano, and the payload's computed roots and counts. Those checks establish consistency with the committed contents. They do not prove that every transaction or state transition is correct; independent fraud detection remains necessary. [Payload verification](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/da/payload.verify-da-payload-against-header.ts#L147).

The earlier recommendation also overstates what a latch accomplishes across members. It only stops a member that sees the differing bytes. Two members each receiving one different variant can still proceed separately; a member might have signed before the second variant arrives. A local latch is useful caution, but it is not network agreement.

## Separate three kinds of disagreement

| What arrived | What it establishes | Recommended response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Different unauthenticated bytes, or bytes failing header checks | A bad candidate or bad relay response. It does not prove the producer made two promises. | Reject that candidate, record the actual source and reason, and continue obtaining authorized data. Preserve usable verified and signed bytes. |
| Two different packages containing identical inner data | A packaging difference. It can still change the exact availability promise. | Before signing, accept only the package named by the producer's authorization or governing commitment. After signing, retain the original exact package. |
| Two incompatible valid producer authorizations or committee promises for the same deployment and header | A real authenticated contradiction, provided the signatures and relevant identities verify. | Preserve both claims as evidence, stop affected new signing, and resolve only through the protocol's authoritative selection or failure procedure. |

“Authenticated” means the appropriate producer or committee key signed the relevant claim, including the deployment, header and exact payload commitment. Knowing which network peer sent bytes is useful attribution, but a relay's identity alone does not authorize it to choose a producer's payload. An honest relay must be able to retransmit the producer's original authorization without becoming its author.

The packaging distinction matters here. Midgard supports both identity and zstd envelopes and hashes the stored envelope. Identical inner bytes can therefore have different outer bytes and different availability commitments. The committee derives its signature commitment from those stored bytes. Recompressing or normalizing after signing changes what was promised. [Envelope modes](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/da-payload-envelope.ts#L278), [commitment derivation](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/peer/signatures.ts#L84).

## The recommended design

1. **Make authorization specific to the header and bytes.** Adopt A8's producer binding: verify that the producer key belongs to the header observed on Cardano, and require its signed authorization for the exact envelope digest, deployment and payload format. A committee relay carries that same authorization. Role checks and connection admission reduce abuse, but neither alone gives permission to select someone else's bytes.

2. **Keep unverified candidates apart from accepted payloads.** Store candidates by deployment, header and payload hash, with the authorization, authenticated sender, verification result and timestamps. The accepted record references a candidate; an incoming packet cannot overwrite it. Bound total bytes, candidates per header, concurrent verification and peer admission. Apply short retention to rejected candidates and longer protocol-based retention to signed data and verified conflict evidence. Reserve admission capacity for authorized recovery so arbitrary first arrivals cannot fill the candidate limit and exclude honest data. Exact numeric limits require load measurements rather than guesses in this decision.

3. **Check candidates before treating differences as a protocol incident.** Cheap size, envelope, deployment and signature checks precede expensive decoding and root computation. Invalid candidates are independently rejected. If the header has not yet been observed, retain only a limited pending candidate and perform no signing. Once an authoritative exact commitment exists, fetch only candidates matching it. Do not choose a variant by popularity, arrival time or lowest hash: none of those establishes authority.

4. **Persist the selected promise before exposing its signature.** Atomically reserve the deployment/header commitment, durably retain its exact bytes, then sign and publish through a restart-safe operation. Once this signer has promised a commitment, it cannot promise a different one for that header. The source already contains a local classifier refusing incompatible signer commitments; preserve that invariant and prove that concurrency and crash recovery enforce it. New junk cannot erase the retained data or prevent its retrieval for a challenge. Genuine authenticated conflict can suspend new decisions while the existing promised data remains available. [Existing signing classifier](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/peer/signatures.ts#L57).

5. **Recover from proof, not elapsed time.** An old fetched-record conflict can clear after recovery checks show that the unwanted candidate lacked authorization or failed the header/commitment checks, and that the selected exact bytes satisfy all required bindings. A signed promise never changes during this repair. If two incompatible authenticated claims remain and there is no protocol rule selecting one, stay unready for that header, retain evidence and escalate. Repeatedly fetching one variant does not disprove the other.

The strongest deeper improvement is to give all members one protocol-authoritative exact availability commitment before they sign. A producer signature makes an attempt attributable but cannot stop a malicious producer signing two attempts. A local database lock only prevents one member signing twice. If different members can still accept different producer authorizations, certificate formation must enforce one commitment with a proven threshold-intersection rule, or Cardano must select one exact commitment in a record bound to the header. The existing committee thresholds and full certificate paths were not audited here, so this note makes no claim that they already guarantee unique selection.

My preferred direction is an explicit exact commitment selected by the protocol, with producer authorization as its admission evidence and immutable local signing as defense in depth. Reuse an existing authoritative attachment if its rules already guarantee unique selection; otherwise specify that rule before launch. Avoid inserting an envelope hash naively into the current header: the payload embeds the header and its hash, so this can create a circular construction. A separately selected availability record bound to the completed header avoids that particular cycle, at the cost of another protocol step. The precise Cardano record and transaction design belongs in a reviewed protocol proposal.

## What other systems teach us

Ethereum's blob networking verifies data against commitments bound to the transaction or signed block. The Deneb rules check the proposer signature, the commitment's inclusion in the block, and the blob proof before marking the sidecar accepted. The applicable lesson is to authenticate the expected data identity and validate incoming bytes before they affect accepted state; Midgard need not adopt Ethereum's cryptography to use that principle. [Ethereum consensus networking](https://ethereum.github.io/consensus-specs/specs/deneb/p2p-interface/#new-blob_sidecar_subnet_id), [EIP-4844 networking](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4844#networking).

Optimism's derivation specification explicitly drops invalid batches, and its Holocene rules warn against turning invalid inputs into critical errors that stall derivation. This supports isolating invalid input from recovery of the valid stream. It does not authorize Midgard to ignore a real contradiction in signed availability promises: Optimism derives from its own ordered L1 inputs and has different failure rules. [OP derivation](https://specs.optimism.io/protocol/derivation.html#batch-queue), [Holocene derivation](https://specs.optimism.io/protocol/holocene/derivation.html#batch-queue).

CometBFT's duplicate-vote evidence requires two conflicting votes from the same validator in the same voting context, valid membership and valid signatures. That is a useful model for evidence: two arbitrary byte strings are not equivalent to two authenticated incompatible claims. [CometBFT evidence specification](https://github.com/cometbft/cometbft/blob/main/spec/consensus/evidence.md#duplicatevoteevidence).

These are design comparisons, not claims that Midgard and these systems have the same security model.

## Alternatives and their costs

| Option | Benefit | Cost and judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First received payload wins permanently | Simple; a later hostile upload cannot displace honest data that arrived first. | Hostile first arrivals exclude honest data. Members can choose different variants. Reject as the policy. |
| Any byte difference permanently stops the header | Conservative after a real ambiguity is observed. | Unverified junk or innocent repackaging can disable useful data; does not establish network agreement. Keep only as the temporary signing safeguard. |
| Validate the first usable candidate, with no exact shared authorization | Filters obvious junk and improves recovery. | Header roots alone do not select one envelope; members may promise different encodings. Insufficient as the complete fix. |
| Normalize every payload into one encoding | Can reduce benign representation differences. | Requires a complete canonicalization rule, consistent compression behavior and proof that every required field is covered. Rewriting signed bytes breaks existing promises. Consider only as a separately reviewed pre-signing protocol change. |
| Bounded candidates, exact authorization, immutable signing and authoritative selection | Rejects poisoning while preserving safety, attribution, honest relays and recoverability. | More storage states and concurrency/recovery logic; protocol selection must be explicit. Recommended. |

For public testnet, treat this as part of the DA admission and recovery work, rather than a separate first-bytes patch. Candidate storage and immutable signed retention can be implemented without changing the meaning of existing signatures. A new authoritative selection rule may require on-chain changes and has to be designed and reviewed separately. Unresolved authenticated ambiguity remains a real stop; it should not be hidden to report uptime.

## Evidence and limits

Read-only source inspection used `rg`, `sed` and `nl` from the repository root on 2026-10-01; completed inspection commands exited 0. An initial lookup of `store.payload-conflict.ts` found no such file; the actual resolver was then located and read. Primary specifications above were consulted on 2026-10-01. Only this decision document was written; no implementation or durable state was changed.

No application tests, builds, live poisoning/recovery drill, threshold-security audit or capacity measurements were run, because the assignment is a research and decision document. Proposed acceptance must cover hostile-before-honest and honest-before-hostile arrivals, innocent repackaging, producer double authorization, bad relay attribution, full candidate-store pressure, signing/upload races, crashes around durable signing, preservation of challenge-serving bytes, and proof-gated recovery after restart. Both storage backends and the peer-fetch path need that coverage. These are acceptance requirements, not verification results.
