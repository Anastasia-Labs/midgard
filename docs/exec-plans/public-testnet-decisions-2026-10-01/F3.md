# F3 — Make capacity pressure produce smaller blocks, not a permanent halt

Recommendation prepared 2026-10-01 from the current working tree. This is a researched implementation proposal, not a completed fix or a measured capacity claim.

**Treat this as P0 and a public-testnet launch blocker. Keep the final size refusal, make the planner budget the complete payload, and add bounded rebuilding of smaller legal candidates before signing.** Add limits and backpressure across the entire pending-transaction lifecycle, publish capacity and stalled-progress signals, and preserve valid transactions that must wait. Separately, implement A6's replacement of per-block full ledger snapshots; block packing alone cannot make that format scale indefinitely.

No malicious behavior is necessary to trigger the described failure. Ordinary traffic can produce a batch that passes the planner's incomplete estimate but cannot be published. The node can remain running while doing the same unsuccessful work repeatedly. Restarting it merely presents the same queue and state to the same selector.

## What is present today

The [batch planner](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/utils/commit-block-planner.plan-earliest-commit-scheduler-due-work.ts#L212) estimates DA bytes as transaction bytes plus a fixed overhead per transaction. The [default overhead is 128 bytes](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/utils/commit-block-planner.commit-scheduler-evidence-key.ts#L64). It also estimates ledger operations, transition steps, L1 commitment size, and build time. Its deterministic selection takes a prefix and stops at the first exceeded budget. It correctly permits [zero selected transactions](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/utils/commit-block-planner.plan-earliest-commit-scheduler-due-work.ts#L277) rather than forcing one above the limit.

What this estimate omits is substantial: the complete resulting ledger snapshot, the different transaction-source and full-preimage representations, deposits/withdrawals/forced transactions, transition and validation traces, witnesses, script program material, and framing. The [final sizing function includes these fields](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header/submission.assert-pre-submit-da-payload-size.ts#L142), using an exact ledger-size aggregate rather than copying the ledger just to count it.

The [final guard](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header/submission.assert-pre-submit-da-payload-size.ts#L194) compares the inner encoding, stored envelope and submitted request against the applicable limits. The hard DA cap is [64 MiB](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/cek-proof.encode-midgard-cek-term-node.ts#L82), and the [compression projection uses a worst-case bound](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/da-payload-sizing.ts#L27). The usable inner capacity is slightly below the outer frame cap; favorable compression must not be assumed.

That guard runs [before pending-submission preparation and signing](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header/submission.submit-tx-backed-commit.ts#L400). Overflow becomes a generic `DatabaseError`. The transaction-backed path and [the no-ordinary-transactions path](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header/submission.submit-deposit-only-commit.ts#L316) both use it. I found no dedicated frame-overflow step-down in these current planner/submission paths. The original memo says that work was commissioned; it is not evidence that the fix exists here.

Deferred processed transactions are [preferred over new mempool transactions](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/utils/commit-block-planner.plan-earliest-commit-scheduler-due-work.ts#L168). That makes preserving a failed selection useful for ordinary submission recovery, but does not change its capacity requirements. If the relevant inputs remain unchanged, the same oversized selection can remain a recurring obstruction. This is a source-based diagnosis, not a reproduced live attack.

Existing queue defenses are useful but distinct. The node has [ingress and durable-admission limits](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/services/config.make-config.ts#L368), with [explicit capacity-full responses](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/commands/listen-router.post-submit-handler.ts#L386). The inspected [durable-admission byte query](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/database/txAdmissions.admit-without-byte-quota.ts#L247) counts sidecar bytes for `queued` and `validating` admissions. That is not a measurement of the complete published payload or of all accepted-but-uncommitted work. Do not assume its cap alone bounds every downstream queue.

The common architectural lesson is layered control: admission limits protect resources, block packing produces legal work, publication/backlog feedback controls sustained load, and fees allocate capacity. None justifies disabling a DA safety limit to maintain headline throughput.

## Recommended immediate fix

### 1. Budget the complete representation

Use the existing canonical size calculations as the shared source of truth. Start with the fixed header/body/framing cost and the size aggregate for the verified starting ledger. Track the resulting ledger aggregate as candidate transactions and L1 events remove and add outputs. Add the actual encoded sources, preimages, traces, witnesses, and deduplicated program material. Reserve space for events required by the selected block window before spending the remaining allowance on ordinary transactions.

Some exact sizes are only available after execution. Use conservative bounds for cheap early selection, then exact sizing of the constructed unsigned candidate. Do not treat a moving average, a measured compression ratio, or another fixed multiplier as a proof of fit. Maintain the existing execution, event-count, L1 transaction, timing, and proof-feasibility limits as independent constraints.

The operational benefit is fewer expensive failed builds. The cost is more explicit accounting and consistent encoder APIs. Existing [aggregate-based final sizing](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header/submission.assert-pre-submit-da-payload-size.ts#L167) provides a foundation; this does not require copying the full ledger on every selection trial.

### 2. Rebuild a smaller candidate within bounded work

Return a dedicated capacity result containing the measured sizes and binding limit. Catch only that result for resizing. Missing source evidence, conflicting program material, invalid roots, database failure, or an ambiguous signed submission must continue through their own safety/recovery paths.

Freeze the verified base, candidate ordering, applicable rules, and source evidence for one planning attempt. For a simple first implementation, try the selected ordinary-transaction prefix, then deterministic halving, down to one and zero ordinary transactions. Each trial must reconstruct its roots, ledger changes, traces, counts, events, and unsigned commitment from the same base. Keep the best legal fitting trial already found and submit only that trial. Optional refinement can reclaim capacity after a fit, provided it stays inside the build-time budget.

For `N` ordinary candidates, halving can be bounded by `ceil(log2(N)) + 2` trial sizes for `N >= 1`. With the [current 10,000-transaction consensus maximum](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/consensus-profile.ts#L343), that is an upper bound of sixteen, not a recommendation to perform sixteen full expensive builds regardless of time. Execution, encoding, memory, source checks, and the remaining operator window must impose tighter work/deadline bounds. A time-limited attempt can defer and resume planned work; it must not revert to the same known-bad full candidate on every tick.

Retain the failed-candidate identity and fit result keyed by verified base root, eligible source set/window, ordered transaction identities, format, and rule configuration. Reuse that knowledge while those facts are unchanged; invalidate it when they change. This prevents each tick or restart from paying for the same overflow again. It must not preserve a refusal after the blocking inputs have changed.

**Do not assume that fewer transactions always means a smaller full-state payload.** A later transaction can consolidate outputs and substantially shrink the resulting ledger. Shared program material also makes incremental costs nonuniform. Halving is a bounded fallback that finds some legal fitting candidates, not a proof that a binary search finds the largest one. Exact-check every trial. Once the estimator includes resulting-state changes, allow bounded examination of independently eligible state-reducing transactions rather than permanently stopping behind one large candidate.

### 3. Isolate trial effects and preserve signed intents

The current worker already begins and [discards a transaction-tree overlay](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header.run-commit-block-header-worker-program.ts#L120), and [native generation cleanup](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header.run-commit-block-header-worker-program.ts#L168) distinguishes retained submission work from discarded work. Resizing must extend that discipline to every affected ledger projection, program-material reference, queue transition, event ownership, and database write. Merely looping the current processing function is not sufficient evidence that all its effects are reversible; it has a [deferred-write option for speculative work](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header.database-operations-program.ts#L734), rather than universally pure construction.

Only the winning candidate should acquire permanent submission ownership. Once a transaction is signed or its submission is ambiguous, reconcile that immutable intent instead of resizing it into another signed block. The size guard remains immediately before durable submission preparation/signing. Revalidate base and source completeness before that boundary; changes invalidate the candidate rather than authorize stale submission.

### 4. Keep obligatory events and zero-transaction cases safe

“Zero ordinary transactions” does not mean “no events.” Deposits, withdrawals, and forced transactions due within the advertised time interval still have to be included. The [current completeness check](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header/submission.run-with-stale-operator-wallet-retry.ts#L114) locks the source tables and requires the exact due set. Keep it.

If that due set cannot fit, consider an earlier legal end time and rebuild the entire candidate, subject to the existing timing/visibility/scheduler rules. Do not retain the later end time while dropping its required events. If no legal boundary can split a mandatory group, report a capacity obstruction explicitly; ordinary-mempool shrinking cannot fix it. Protocol event limits and forced-inclusion deadlines must be designed so an admitted obligatory event cannot create an impossible required batch.

An event-free candidate retains the verified state root and has correct empty event commitments; it must not substitute an empty ledger root. Empty blocks can advance protocol time where allowed, but a stream of empty blocks with pending ordinary transactions is not healthy user throughput.

## Queue admission, fairness, and fees

Bound resources from receipt through validation, accepted mempool, deferred processed work, and eventual commitment. Account for transaction bytes, sidecars, retained proof/program material, memory, database storage, and estimated validation work; deduplicate shared objects without double reservation. Release reservations on real terminal transitions or confirmed handoff, not simply because an item passed validation into another queue. Use the existing ingress/backlog mechanisms as a starting point rather than introducing unrelated competing queues.

When capacity is temporarily full, reject new admission clearly before promising durable acceptance, with a retry hint and transaction-status lookup for response-loss ambiguity. Keep already accepted valid transactions and their age/order; mark capacity deferral distinctly from invalidity. A transaction that cannot fit the remaining space of this block can fit the next one. Reject a newly submitted transaction as permanently oversized only against a published, stable per-transaction rule that can establish that result, not because today's ledger or queue is temporarily large.

Use deterministic oldest-eligible ordering first, preserving transaction dependencies. The [current dependency ordering](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/mpf/mempool-order.ts#L75) identifies producer/consumer relationships. If an independent oversized candidate is deferred, later independent work can be considered within a bounded scan; a child cannot be included without its required predecessor. Make deferral visible and prevent unbounded starvation through age-aware scheduling and progress metrics. Under sustained demand beyond capacity, no scheduler can promise immediate inclusion for everyone; admission and fee policies must describe that limit honestly.

Fees should ultimately cover execution/proof work and attributable data publication, with congestion feedback and wallet-visible limits. Today's [minimum fee is based on canonical transaction length](../../../demo/midgard-validation/src/phase-a.validate-phase-asingle.ts#L227), and the [node configuration defaults both coefficients to zero](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/services/config.make-config.ts#L351). This establishes configurable behavior, not that the current deployed profile actually charges zero.

A richer fee rule is a coordinated protocol/wallet/proof change; do not unilaterally add unexpected charges to accepted signed transactions. Price the new work caused by the transaction and the costs of retaining/publishing it. Repeating the entire old ledger makes cost attribution especially awkward, another reason to implement A6. For launch, use explicit measured fee/admission settings and hard capacity bounds; refine dynamic fees without delaying the P0 packing fix. Fees cannot guarantee correctness against a funded attacker.

## The separate state-size cliff

The payload contains the **resulting** ledger, as explained in [A6](A6.md:15). Growing state consumes more of every frame even with little new traffic. Eventually unchanged state alone may exceed the current cap.

There is an important precision point: an oversized empty candidate does not logically prove that every possible candidate is oversized. A legal consolidation or withdrawal can reduce the resulting snapshot enough to offset its own transaction/event overhead. Whether any such candidate exists, is eligible, and can be found inside bounded work is a separate question. Do not declare a mathematically universal halt based solely on an empty-frame check.

Nevertheless, relying on users to consolidate at the cliff is not a scalable architecture or a recovery guarantee. Some ledgers have no sufficiently shrinking eligible candidate; mandatory events can add further work. No selection algorithm can publish a legal candidate whose required bytes exceed the cap. Raising the cap, compression, congestion fees, or restarting postpones or changes this limit without removing the full-state dependency.

Implement A6's transaction/event inputs, retained proof evidence, independent replay, and separately authenticated checkpoints. Until that passes its proof and recovery gates, keep exact frame guards and explicit growth warnings. Any interim capacity policy must preserve L1-accepted deposits/forced obligations; stopping ordinary API requests alone cannot undo events already accepted on Cardano.

## Metrics, readiness, and thresholds

Publish on `/metrics` and in `/readyz` detail: exact candidate inner bytes, envelope/request upper bounds, applicable limits, state-only baseline, resulting ledger bytes, available space, initial/final transaction counts, resized/deferred counts, attempts/build duration, oldest accepted transaction age, last productive commitment, and the current capacity reason. Report estimate error so the 128-byte model cannot silently be replaced by another inaccurate heuristic.

Carry these observations from the [worker thread](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/block-commitment.build-and-submit-commitment-block-action.ts#L187) to the parent that owns the [Prometheus exporter](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/commands/listen.run-node.ts#L404). A gauge created only inside the worker is insufficient. Include valid base/header identity and observation age, and clear old refusal reasons when new evidence demonstrates progress.

For comparison across envelope modes, frame pressure can be the maximum of inner bytes divided by effective inner limit, stored-envelope upper bound divided by frame cap, and request upper bound divided by frame cap. Distinguish an intentionally full busy block from a nearly full **state-only** baseline. High utilization with steady inclusion is efficient operation; high fixed-state use threatens every future block.

Suggested interim warnings are 80% and 90% for the state-only baseline, with exact-limit refusal unchanged. These are proposed operational starting points, not measured safe growth horizons, and should be reconciled with D5's threshold ruling. More useful critical triggers include inability to fit the mandatory event set, no productive legal candidate after bounded trials, and oldest accepted work exceeding the published service target. Derive backlog thresholds from measured drain rate and acceptable waiting time rather than arbitrary transaction count alone.

Keep liveness healthy while the process is correctly reporting and managing a capacity problem. Report unready when capacity prevents its promised work or service limits are exceeded. A successful resize that keeps including transactions is a detail/counter, not an automatic readiness failure. Preserve status/read access and safe allowed operations; reject additions only at their actual admission/resource boundary. Do not let readiness become a restart trigger.

## Tradeoffs and release gates

| Choice                                            | Benefit                                                     | Limitation                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Keep refusing the oversized candidate unchanged   | Prevents publishing unavailable bytes                       | Leaves the avoidable repeated-selection halt                                    |
| Lower every block to a tiny fixed count           | Quick and conservative                                      | Wastes throughput; still cannot bound variable traces/state or mandatory events |
| Increase frame cap or assume compression          | Delays some refusals                                        | Increases transfer/memory cost and retains the architectural cliff              |
| Clear or silently evict accepted transactions     | May temporarily shorten a queue                             | Breaks acceptance expectations; repeated traffic recreates the obstruction      |
| Complete accounting plus bounded isolated fitting | Uses available capacity and preserves safety                | Requires careful replay/isolation and explicit progress reporting               |
| Replace full-state DA publication                 | Removes the fundamental dependence on total unchanged state | Needs coordinated replay, checkpoint, retention, and fault-proof work           |

The immediate fix should ship first, alongside observability; complete-lifecycle admission bounds are also required for public-testnet resource protection. A6 is the structural launch work. Dynamic fee refinement follows measurement and must not substitute for either.

Required future acceptance cases include an initially overflowing candidate that produces a smaller valid block; exact estimator/encoder agreement across compression and CBOR boundaries; oversized oldest work followed by eligible smaller work; dependencies across a resized boundary; forced/deposit/withdrawal completeness after end-time changes; large traces/program material; ordinary-zero and truly empty candidates; a later consolidation making a larger prefix smaller; rollback/crash between trials; restart preserving accepted work and known failed-candidate state; no duplicate signing/submission; queue caps across validation-to-mempool handoff; and pending-user age becoming unready despite empty blocks. After A6, use state larger than the old cap and prove small and empty blocks still publish and remain challengeable.

Measure usable capacity and verification/build time on the intended deployment before setting numerical transaction, backlog, or throughput targets. No exact UTxO-count cliff, bytes-per-entry, attack cost, daily growth rate, or achievable throughput was measured for this report.
