# E2 — An event included too early must not force another operator to apply it twice

Recommendation researched on 2026-10-01 against the current working tree. No
validator, node, specification or running service was changed.

**Keep responsibility attached to the invalid ancestor and require operators
to validate their parent before building. Define event consumption consistently
across omission, duplication and replay, with authenticated prior-consumption
state. A known-invalid ancestor that has already merged requires a protocol
incident and an explicit recovery decision; its age must never turn it into a
valid base automatically.**

The important correction to the original question is that early inclusion is
already a provable fault. Adding that proof alone does not close the gap.
Neither does an off-chain instruction to skip a previously included event:
today's omission proof can still punish that skipped event. There is no
automatic productive continuation from the stated settled-invalid situation
that preserves both the current challenge deadline and the owner's existing
descendant-responsibility ruling.

## The situation in plain English

A deposit, withdrawal instruction or forced transaction order has an
authenticated time when it belongs in an L2 block. That is its eligibility
time, not necessarily the instant somebody first sees the transaction. For
deposit and withdrawal history, admission fixes it to the submission
transaction's upper time bound plus the configured waiting period. See
[event admission](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/event-history/order-facts.ak#L145).

Each L2 block owns a consecutive time interval. An event belongs to the block
whose interval starts before the eligibility time and ends at or after it.
The starting boundary is excluded and the ending boundary is included. An
operator cannot choose a different event time. See
[the timing rule](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/event-history/horizon.ak#L7).

For illustration, use arbitrary time units rather than a deployment parameter:

| Item | Time or interval | What should happen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ancestor A | `(0, 100]` | Must not include an event eligible at 150 |
| Event E | Eligibility time 150 | Belongs to the later interval |
| Descendant B | `(100, 200]` | Normally must include E |

Suppose A wrongly includes E anyway. Its deposit root records that event and
its claimed state already reflects the credit. Nobody completes a proof in
time, so A merges into the confirmed state. Its settlement output preserves
the old event root.

Now an operator trying to construct B encounters two conflicting rules:

- Include E: the duplicate proof sees E in A's confirmed settlement and B's
  live event root, and accuses B of repeating it.
- Leave E out: the omission proof sees an authentic event eligible in B's
  interval and absent from B's root, and accuses B of leaving it out.

The same danger affects withdrawals and forced orders, though their transition
and eligibility details differ. A deposit alone is enough to demonstrate the
conflicting predicates without depending on those additional details.

## Exactly what the current predicates establish

The omission predicate authenticates the event's L1 history, checks that its
eligibility time falls in the accused block's interval, and proves the event
key is absent from that block's counted root. It receives no previous event
consumption root, ancestor-membership witness or settled-event exception. That
missing input is visible in both its function signature and its final two
conditions. See
[deposit/withdrawal omission](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/transition-trace/timed-history.ak#L54).
The forced-order version similarly combines timing/transaction-validity
eligibility with absence from the forced-order root. See
[forced-order omission](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/transition-trace/proof.ak#L2067).

The duplicate family authenticates the challenged live header and its event
leaf, then authenticates a distinct settlement and the same key under the same
event-root domain. It does not check whether the settled inclusion was in the
correct time interval. Its comments assume a settled event cannot be due
again, which follows only if that earlier inclusion was valid. See
[the stated duplicate rule](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/cross-block-duplicate-event/step-01.ak#L26)
and [the actual settlement comparison](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/cross-block-duplicate-event/step-02.ak#L49)
(both since deleted). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

**Early inclusion already has its own fault predicate.**
`OutOfWindowSourceEvent` authenticates the event's L1 time and its membership in
the accused header, then checks that the time is outside that header's interval.
For the example, that accuses A directly. It covers early and late copies. See
[deposit timing fault](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/transition-trace/timed-history.ak#L96),
[withdrawal timing fault](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/transition-trace/timed-history.ak#L129)
and [forced-order timing fault](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/fraud-proofs/transition-trace/proof.ak#L2097).

Merge is optimistic. It checks that fraud has not been marked, checks DA status,
header structure, commitment matching and maturity, then advances the confirmed
state to the header's claimed UTxO root. It does not independently open every
event and validate its eligibility. A successful merge transaction is therefore
not evidence that all included event times were correct. See
[merge](../../../onchain/aiken/validators/state-queue.ak#L448).

The contradictory accusation is conditional on usable evidence, not an
unconditional claim that every such descendant will be slashed. In particular:

1. A's settlement output must still exist, with an openable membership witness
   for E, for the current duplicate proof to use it.
2. B must still be challengeable, and the authentic event/history evidence must
   remain available, for the omission proof to complete.
3. With maturity duration M, there is a possible overlap after `100 + M` and
   before `200 + M`: A can already be merged while B remains challengeable.
   Actual transactions also need the permitted finite validity bounds.

For a deposit/withdrawal eligible at 150, retirement requires confirmed time to
have reached 150. With only A confirmed through 100, that retirement condition
is not met, so A's early inclusion does not automatically erase the L1 evidence
needed against B. See
[retirement's independent finalized-time check](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/event-history/retirement.ak#L109).
This strengthens the example: the early merged root and the still-authentic
due event can coexist.

These are source-derived conditions, not a reproduced emulator attack.

## What “honest descendant” means under the existing owner ruling

[Issue #683](https://github.com/Anastasia-Labs/midgard/issues/683) records the
owner's ruling that operators which build on fraudulent blocks are responsible
for doing so. Operators receive and check their ancestors. Correction of a
fraudulent chain removes descendants and slashes each removed descendant's own
operator; it does not permit charging an unrelated operator or naming the
ancestor's operator for every removal.

Under that ruling, an operator who verifies A and finds the early inclusion
must not commit B on top of A. “I applied B's own due events correctly” is not
enough to make B honest: choosing an invalid base is itself disallowed. The
duplicate family's comments about a forced honest live descendant conflict
with that broad responsibility rule. Those comments must be reconciled
with the accepted policy when the specification is changed.

This is why simply making omission ignore any ancestor inclusion would be a
policy change, not a harmless repair. It would let a malicious early inclusion
determine later event handling while an operator knowingly extends the invalid
ancestor.

There is a safe action: **do not publish that descendant**. There may not be a
safe action that also advances block production. Those are different promises.

## What the operator should do before and after merge

**While A remains challengeable:** fetch the full payload, authenticate all
commitments, replay the events against their L1 authority and timing, and refuse
to build on A if it is invalid. Submit the timing fault against A promptly,
complete the proof before the deadline, correct the queue, then build the
replacement suffix from the last valid base. Removing the invalid suffix must
restore events to pending status; it must not leave behind a permanent
“already handled” marker for a removed block.

Payload availability and matching the UTxO root alone are insufficient. The
operator must check the event roots, counts, eligibility and execution. This
connects to E1/E4's foreign-block verification work, but E2 does not implement or
claim that work is complete.

**After A has merged:** first distinguish missing data from demonstrated
invalidity. Missing data warrants bounded retrieval/replay and an unavailable
status. Demonstrated early inclusion is a correctness incident. Preserve the
evidence, report that block production is unsafe, stop producing descendants,
and keep diagnostic/recovery services running. A restart must preserve that
refusal. Repeating the same calculation or waiting longer cannot fix A.

The deposit/withdrawal proof capture explicitly requires completion before the
accused header's earliest merge boundary. The helper's documented completion
rule does not extend that deadline because a thread exists. See
[capture/completion limits](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/event-history/horizon.ak#L16)
and [the enforced payload-capture limit](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/event-history/order-facts.ak#L279).
Do not promise that retaining the bytes lets a node challenge a merged A later.

Recovering this already-settled incident requires an explicit protocol recovery
or deployment migration that reconciles the confirmed root, event consumption
and any settlement payments already made. This review does not assume such a
mechanism exists. An automated local database rewrite would leave the node
disagreeing with L1; a second event application could create extra credit or a
second payout. Neither is recovery.

## Alternatives and their consequences

| Option | What it achieves | Problem or cost | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Add an early-inclusion proof | Blames the first incorrect inclusion | That proof already exists; expiry still matters | Preserve and exercise existing proof |
| Reinclude the event in its proper window | Honors the isolated omission rule | Repeats an effect already claimed in A; duplicate proof remains | Reject |
| Skip it whenever any ancestor root contains it | Avoids repeated execution | Needs a changed on-chain omission rule; accepts invalid bases under today's #683 ruling | Reject as an isolated patch |
| Make duplicates depend on which copy has the correct time | Improves attribution while both copies are live | Does not reverse the settled financial effect; prior timing evidence can disappear | Useful clarification, insufficient recovery |
| Treat settled roots as authoritative despite known-invalid execution | Allows descendants to advance using prior-consumption state | Changes #683 and explicitly tolerates finalized fraud; cannot restore lost funds | Requires a new owner ruling; not recommended here |
| Enforce all event timing on chain before accepting/finalizing a root | Prevents this particular early-inclusion incident without relying on a challenger | Must authenticate every committed leaf's time and complete coverage; substantial cost and protocol change | Longer-term alternative if measured fit justifies it |
| Shared consumption commitments, validated parents and timely challenges | Makes normal execution, replay and fault rules agree | Adds protocol state/proofs; cannot repair fraud that already escaped finality | Recommended design, with explicit incident handling |

Checking one selected event's time at merge is not the full on-chain alternative.
The check must prove that every committed event is authentic and eligible, with
none hidden in the root. A compact certificate or aggregate range would itself
need that completeness guarantee. Its feasibility and Cardano execution cost
were not measured here.

## The recommended protocol design

Define an authenticated map of event handling carried forward with the ledger
state. Its entries use the event kind and stable L1 identity, and bind the first
handling block and disposition. The small commitment to this map belongs in the
state/header and its transition proofs; each node stores enough data to generate
membership and absence witnesses. A local SQL table or a collection of old
settlement outputs cannot substitute for that commitment.

For every event, use one rule across node execution, watcher replay, committee
verification and fault proofs:

- A first handling is permitted only in the authenticated eligible interval,
  with the correct source content and verdict.
- The event's ledger effects occur once, and its handled identity is inserted
  through a proved transition. Withdrawal rejection/refund and forced-order
  rejection must have specified dispositions, rather than disappearing from
  accounting because they produced no successful transaction.
- A repeat handling is disallowed when the parent state already proves the
  identity handled.
- An omission accusation must establish that the event is due and still
  unhandled under the admissible parent state, as well as absent from the
  accused block. Claimed ancestor membership by itself is not proof of a valid
  first handling.
- A known-invalid parent remains inadmissible even if its handled map includes
  the event. The map is an accounting commitment, not a pardon for an early or
  otherwise fraudulent ancestor.

The current header contains per-block event roots and ledger roots, but no
explicit cumulative handled-event commitment. Confirmed state similarly stores
the ledger root and header/time identities. This is therefore a protocol
extension with builder, DA, replay and recovery consequences, not an extra
boolean in the omission helper. See
[current header fields](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/ledger-state.ak#L56)
and [current confirmed-state fields](../../../onchain/aiken/lib/midgard/ledger-state.ak#L114).

This map also addresses a separate weakness noted in the duplicate-family
source: once an old settlement output is consumed, a much later duplicate loses
its present on-chain witness. Persistent state commitment preserves the
anti-repeat fact without keeping every settlement output unspent forever.
It does not eliminate historical data storage: map proofs, replay and snapshot
verification must remain recoverable, and deletion requires a proven monotone
event-processing boundary rather than a guessed age. Coordinate this with B7
and E3.

While an invalid ancestor is pending, correction restores the map from the last
valid base. After invalid finalization, the incident rule above applies. That
retains #683's attribution and avoids quietly promising recovery that the
challenge/finality mechanism cannot provide.

## Why this is the best tradeoff

The direct fix is consistency between eligibility, previous handling,
execution and blame, combined with enforcing the already-agreed obligation to
validate ancestors. It preserves exact-once effects and independent proof
construction. It also makes the assumption behind optimistic finalization
explicit: an invalid claim needs a timely effective challenge. No finite
challenge period can both guarantee irreversible settlement and permit ordinary
late undoing of a missed fraud indefinitely.

The OP Stack likewise specifies a challenger that compares claims against its
own synchronized rollup state and responds promptly before its clock expires.
It specifies emergency authority separately through pause/blacklist/fallback
roles. Those are explicit mechanisms, not a rule that time corrects invalid
execution. This comparison supports separating normal proof recovery from
exceptional protocol recovery; it does not imply that Midgard has, or should
silently add, those permissions.
[OP honest challenger](https://specs.optimism.io/fault-proof/stage-one/honest-challenger-fdg.html),
[OP bridge emergency roles](https://specs.optimism.io/fault-proof/stage-one/bridge-integration.html).

For testnet, require the before-merge detection/refusal/challenge/removal flow
before multi-operator production is considered safe. Use a challenge period
that permits the measured complete proof lifecycle, independent funded watchers,
and visible deadline alerts. A longer period reduces missed-challenge risk; it
does not resolve inconsistent rules or waive invalid-ancestor responsibility.

The already-settled example remains a safety incident until an explicit recovery
mechanism is designed and authorized. If the owner instead wants a guaranteed
productive descendant after known fraud has finalized, the finality or #683
policy must change explicitly. That cannot honestly be delivered as a small
predicate patch under the current instructions.
