> Note (deleted 2026-10-03, #752): speculative commit mode, the foreign-tip reconciliation table `foreign_tip_reconciliations`, the T2 foreign-event reconciliation workers, the foreign DA reconciliation fiber and the commit-time foreign-tip gate no longer exist. ## Implemented accepted decision — 2026-10-01

The regular retention sweeper now prunes `foreign_tip_reconciliations` in batches of at most 128, repeating batches within one sweep until a batch comes back short or 64 batches have run, and is the table's only pruner: the commit gate no longer deletes evidence. It uses the same `MIDGARD_RETENTION_WINDOW` cutoff as DA retention and runs independently of `RETENTION_DAYS`. A row must be strictly older than that cutoff and strictly before the event-history owner's retained rollback anchor. Its window must also be covered by the owner's complete authenticated event index and by forced-transaction ingestion. Each successful tx-order reconcile on the main thread records a monotone watermark in `Globals`: the time its L1 fetch started, or the barrier's inclusive upper bound when that is earlier. On chain an order's inclusion time is its validity end plus the event wait, so an order not yet minted when the fetch started cannot fall at or before that time. The sweeper only reads this watermark; it makes no L1 call and never takes the L1 control plane. The ingested-through time is the earlier of the owner's indexed-through time and that watermark. These are separate conditions; a resolution timestamp or elapsed wall-clock time cannot prove completeness. See pruning implementation (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.retention.ts:47`), watermark (`demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/fetch-and-insert-tx-order-utxos.tx-order-utx-oto-entry.ts:231`, since deleted) and [sweeper integration](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/retention-sweeper.ts#L250). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

The real owner now publishes its retained anchor and the anchor's time using the same verified slot mapping that already supplies its indexed-through time. The canonical history consumer materializes and checks event rows before the owner opens Ready; the first-start replay retains admitted and retired incarnations, and forward processing materializes each changed incarnation. This existing authenticated consumer progress supplies the completeness prerequisite. No new wall-clock completion flag or database migration was added. Deletion enters a checked Ready producer transaction, takes the existing history-authority lock, rechecks coverage, and requires the retained anchor to still match the database. Missing retention authority or a changed anchor conservatively keeps the rows. See owner coverage (`demo/midgard-node/src/services/event-history-owner.coverage.ts:4`, since deleted), complete origin replay (`demo/midgard-node/src/l1-event-history-list-replay.ts:255`, since deleted), consumer materialization (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/eventHistoryMaterialization.ts:348`, since deleted), runtime consumer wiring (`demo/midgard-node/src/services/event-history-runtime.ts:271`, since deleted), and producer fencing (`demo/midgard-node/src/services/event-history-producer.ts:187`, since deleted). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

Every row keeps the global pins: the confirmed head, live queue headers, active journal references to the foreign header or replaced base, signed abandoned journals that may still land, and either prepared or applied recovery-plan references. Recovery holds already keep the owner's anchor back, so the anchor condition also preserves their required history. Applied recovery-plan rows and signed abandoned journals remain conservative durable pins; this change does not introduce a new expiry policy for them. Every row, whatever its status or deployment, can go only once events are ingested past its window by the full horizon: its block end must be older than the ingested-through time above minus `requiredRetentionMs`. Beyond those pins and that cutoff, the rule depends on the row:

- A resolved row of this deployment and consensus profile is kept while an unfinished event lies in its window or is assigned to its foreign header. Projected deposits and withdrawals still needing finalization remain pins, including invalid withdrawal verdicts. A deposit with no projected header also pins in any status, `consumed` included: `consumed` says only that an L2 transaction spent its output, and the commit gate still counts it while it has no header.
- An awaiting row of this deployment and profile must also hold neither unconditional verdict (`invalid` nor `foreign_event_present_requires_finalization`), its header's event roots and counts must agree, and the same unfinished-event pins apply. A window the commit gate could still have to lift, or a verdict no window lifts, therefore keeps its row.
- A row from another deployment or profile needs only the shared cutoff. This deployment's events cannot be in it, so the event pins do not apply. A node without a deployment manifest (a derived bundle) treats every row of its profile as its own and prunes it under the first two rules.

If a fresh L1 view is unavailable, foreign evidence is not pruned. A sweeper whose last good L1 view is older than `L1_VIEW_FATAL_MS` stops sweeping and raises `retention_l1_view_stale` as a readiness reason; it keeps reading L1, the first good view clears the reason, and the node never exits for it. See [stale-view reason](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/retention-sweeper.ts#L107). Until the first tx-order reconcile after start records the watermark, foreign evidence is not pruned; a failed reconcile leaves the watermark where it was. If the owner is unavailable or refuses the producer, deletion stops for that sweep and a warning names the cause and the rows already removed. A DA payload prune failure no longer skips the foreign-evidence prune; the DA failure is still raised after it. Pruned-row counts are included in the existing sweep log.

Commit-time retained reconciliation now selects actionable windows in SQL, scoped to this deployment and consensus profile, and reads at most 64 rows per query. It visits every page before returning, using immutable start time, end time and header hash as its continuation key. Awaiting evidence is still visited, and resolved evidence is visited when late-indexed awaiting events appear in its window. Every in-scope row is read and decoded one at a time: a row of any status that no longer decodes is reported with its window and stored verdict and is judged on those, so one bad row cannot fail the page and a corrupt resolved row still gates its window. Resolved rows are therefore decoded on every pass. The page size limits each read's memory; it does not allow the commit gate to skip an unseen awaiting row. See bounded query (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.retention.ts:181`) and complete traversal (`demo/midgard-node/src/workers/t2-foreign-event-reconciliation.reconcile-overdue-awaiting-events-against-retained-foreign-tips.ts:81`). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

## What this table is for

A node can start preparing a block on one chain tip, then discover that a different block has taken that position. Before discarding its prepared work, it saves the newly observed block's identity and the period that block covers. This provides a durable reminder to check whether deposits, withdrawals, or forced transactions in that period were already handled by the other block. A forced transaction is a transaction submitted through Cardano that the rollup must eventually include.

“Foreign” here means a tip different from the candidate's expected base. The recording function compares block identities; it does not itself prove that the block came from a different operator. The record is saved before the candidate is invalidated. See mismatch recording, lines 148–205 (`demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/speculative-commit-builder.persist-authenticated-foreign-tip-mismatch.ts:148`) and record-before-invalidation, lines 222–238 (`demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/speculative-commit-builder.persist-authenticated-foreign-tip-mismatch.ts:222`). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

Each record holds:

- The newly observed block's fingerprint, the old base's fingerprint, and the complete encoded block header.
- The exact deployment and protocol identity, so evidence from another deployment cannot be silently reused.
- The block's start and end times, and its declared deposits, forced transactions, and withdrawals: their counts and cryptographic fingerprints.
- Whether verification is still awaiting evidence or has completed, the reason for any hold, and lifecycle timestamps.
- For a verified block with non-empty event lists, the complete encoded data-availability payload, its format version, and a fingerprint of those bytes. Data availability means the block's contents can be obtained and checked independently.

The schema and types explicitly include these fields. There is one row per foreign block fingerprint, rather than a new row for every retry. See record types, lines 28–123 (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.parse-evidence.ts:28`), [table schema, lines 320–344](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/database/migrations/sql/0001_initial_schema.sql#L320), and insert conflict handling, lines 273–289 (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.record-mismatch.ts:273`). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

The record survives restarts and survives the chain tip moving ahead. That matters because the event indexer may discover a deposit or withdrawal late. The node deliberately revisits even resolved records when a newly discovered event falls inside their old window. Without the retained evidence, it could lose the ability to decide whether that event was already included. See late-event replay, lines 106–134 (`demo/midgard-node/src/workers/t2-foreign-event-reconciliation.reconcile-overdue-awaiting-events-against-retained-foreign-tips.ts:106`). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

“Resolved” therefore means the evidence was verified, not that nobody will ever need it again.

## What prunes it today, and when

Nothing automatically prunes this table in the inspected source. Its database API offers reads, updates, insertion, and a whole-table `clear`; that clear performs a `TRUNCATE`, and its only discovered caller is test setup. It is not a retention policy. See API exports (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.ts:15`), clear definition (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.mark-resolved.ts:306`), [truncate implementation](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/database/utils/common.ts#L34), and [test setup](../../../demo/midgard-node/tests/deposit-flow-emulator-shared.ts#L609). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

The regular retention sweeper prunes the separate `da_payloads` table. It does not include `foreign_tip_reconciliations`. This distinction matters: resolving a reconciliation copies the verified payload bytes into the reconciliation row itself. Deleting the original `da_payloads` row leaves this copy behind. See [sweeper action, lines 155–170](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/fibers/retention-sweeper.ts#L155), payload persistence, lines 224–245 (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.mark-resolved.ts:224`), and replay prefers that retained copy, lines 123–150 (`demo/midgard-node/src/workers/t2-foreign-event-reconciliation.reconcile-retained-foreign-tip-entry.ts:123`). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

Consequently its effective horizon today is unlimited. There is also a growing processing cost: normal commit work calls retained reconciliation replay, which selects and decodes the entire table before checking which resolved windows still need action. See [commit call, lines 567–575](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/workers/commit-block-header.database-operations-program.ts#L567) and full history query, lines 58–70 (`demo/midgard-node/src/database/foreignTipReconciliations.mark-resolved.ts:58`). <!-- doc-links:historical -->

The earlier memo says pruning was being implemented. That claim is not present in this checkout's table API or sweeper; the current-source finding takes precedence.

## Which horizon to use

Use the shared `MIDGARD_RETENTION_WINDOW.requiredRetentionMs`. It is computed as block maturity plus half that maturity for the worst-case proof process. Age is measured from the block's end time, rather than when this node first saved or resolved the row. See [shared derivation, lines 48–70](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/retention-window.assert-da-challenge-window-within-maturity.ts#L48) and [block-end cutoff](../../../demo/midgard-node/src/database/retention-policy.ts#L82).

The exact number depends on the compiled deployment profile:

| Profile                                      | Block maturity | Derived minimum horizon after the block ends |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `preprod-testing`, selected in this checkout | 15 minutes     | 22.5 minutes                                 |
| `preprod-public`                             | 7 days         | 10.5 days                                    |

See [selected profile](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/generated-deployment-profiles.ts#L255), [testing maturity](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/generated-deployment-profiles.ts#L97), and [public maturity](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/generated-deployment-profiles.ts#L53). A public testnet must use the number derived from its actual deployed profile. Hardcoding the testing value would be unsafe.

The separate 15-day transport setting is operational headroom; the current ordinary payload pruning decision uses the derived challengeability horizon and keeps exceptions for the confirmed head and blocks still in the live queue. A challenged block can remain live past its nominal clock deadline. See [transport setting](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/da-transport.da-libp2p-runtime-manifest.ts#L48), [pruning decision, lines 113–133](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/retention-window.da-retention-prune-decision.ts#L113), and [why live challenges remain pinned, lines 76–90](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/retention-window.assert-da-challenge-window-within-maturity.ts#L76).

## Ballpark storage

A pending record, or a resolved record whose event commitments are empty, is small: budget roughly **1–3 KiB per row**, including modest index overhead. That is a planning allowance inferred from the fields, not a PostgreSQL measurement. Header size and unusually long hold reasons can change it.

A resolved non-empty record costs that small amount **plus the complete stored payload**. Estimate using the stored encoded bytes, including any transport compression, rather than assuming that the decoded payload and disk bytes have identical sizes. PostgreSQL compression, large-value storage, update overhead, backups, and replication make actual disk cost different again.

For a concrete example, assume one distinct reconciliation is recorded every ten seconds, and every one eventually needs a payload. This is an illustration of encounter rate, not a claimed network rate. It gives 8,640 rows per day:

| Assumed average payload bytes retained per row | Payload copies added per day with today's unlimited retention | Normal 22.5-minute window | Normal 10.5-day window |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| No payload; use 2 KiB metadata allowance       | About 17 MiB of metadata                                      | About 270 KiB             | About 177 MiB          |
| 1 MiB                                          | 8.44 GiB                                                      | 135 MiB                   | 88.6 GiB               |
| 10 MiB                                         | 84.4 GiB                                                      | 1.32 GiB                  | 886 GiB                |

The window columns assume steady progress, immediate pruning after eligibility, and no extra recovery or live-queue pins. The payload rows omit the comparatively small metadata allowance. A slower encounter rate reduces these numbers proportionally. If only half the records need a payload, halve the payload component. At one 1 MiB record every ten seconds, leaving the table unpruned adds approximately 3 TiB in a year before database and backup overhead. These are arithmetic estimates dated 2026-10-01, not measured sizes.

The current payload ceiling is 64 MiB, so assuming every resolved row is tiny would be particularly misleading. See [payload ceiling](../../../demo/midgard-core/src/cek-proof.encode-midgard-cek-term-node.ts#L82). A6 addresses whether the payload format itself should change; A3 should retain whatever evidence the active format actually requires.

## Recommended deletion rule and its safeguards

Delete a record only when **all** of the following are established:

1. It is resolved with evidence bound to the correct deployment, header, and protocol. An old unresolved record, a known invalid header, or an event known to be included but still awaiting finalization must not disappear just because it aged out.
2. Its block end is strictly older than the shared challengeability cutoff.
3. A fresh authenticated Cardano view proves the block is neither the confirmed head nor in the live queue. Keep evidence needed by an open availability challenge, including when the ordinary age deadline has passed.
4. The event-history owner has completely processed the block's event window, and that window lies outside the supported rollback/replay range. Looking for zero awaiting events in the database is insufficient: an event that has not yet been indexed is invisible to that query.
5. No pending finalization, correction, recovery operation, or ledger catch-up still depends on the record or its payload. Either retain the bytes here or retain an authenticated, durable replacement and pin it for every outstanding consumer.

This is a recommended rule to implement, not a claim that these safeguards already exist for this table. The current history owner exposes the authenticated point and time through which it has processed events; recovery checks bind work to a locked history checkpoint. Those are useful foundations for making pruning atomic with indexing and recovery. See history coverage, lines 176–187 (`demo/midgard-node/src/services/event-history-owner.make-event-history-owner.ts:176`, since deleted) and [checkpoint locking, lines 185–199](https://github.com/Anastasia-Labs/midgard/blob/e30aab4f362c272fa89e6726317bdea9e5979ecc/demo/midgard-node/src/database/eventHistoryRecoveryPlans.prepare-history-recovery-plan.ts#L185) (that file is since deleted). Deletion must recheck those facts under the relevant writer/checkpoint protection so a concurrent replay cannot recreate a need after the sweeper has discarded its evidence. <!-- doc-links:historical -->

The tradeoff is deliberate: healthy operation becomes bounded, while slow indexing or recovery can temporarily retain more than the normal horizon. Report the retained row count, retained payload bytes, oldest age, and why records remain pinned. A storage ceiling should alert the operator and expose failed progress; it should not silently delete correctness evidence. Longer outages that exceed every supported retention window need the catch-up design in E3, rather than indefinite retention hidden in this table.

I agree with your accepted recommendation once these conditions are included. A simple `resolved AND block_end_time < cutoff` patch would address growth but would not establish safe deletion for late-indexed events and recovery.
