# D3 — Preserve promised data while recovering the committee's chain view

Recommendation prepared on 2026-10-01 against current checkout HEAD `3027be19cb3bc740f83af0e155e34ab38c7cf894`. This is a decision document, not an implemented repair or a measured live recovery result. It complements [B4: proof-based recovery](B4.md) and [A5: dependency-scoped journal containment](A5.md).

## Recommended ruling

Approve recovery that reconstructs the correct chain state from authenticated history. Preserve a stopped state when the necessary proof is missing or contradictory. At the same time, keep supplying the exact bytes and historical signature records for existing availability promises, provided their deployment identity and cryptographic binding can still be checked.

Separate three permissions:

1. **Serve existing data.** A peer may retrieve the bytes for a specific frozen commitment, even while the serving node is recovering its chain view. The response must disclose that current chain status is uncertain.
2. **Make a new committee decision.** New availability signatures, attestation applications, and assertions that a header is currently canonical remain held until their relevant chain evidence is trustworthy.
3. **Answer a challenge on Cardano.** This can continue through an independently healthy responder that authenticates the live challenge, its commitment and its own transaction resources. It must not bypass a disputed chain view or an unresolved wallet journal merely because the payload was previously signed.

This is stronger than either permanent shutdown or unconditional automatic clearing. It preserves data needed to defend old promises and investigate faults, while retaining the safety reason for stopping new decisions.

The earlier brief calls serving previously signed data “pure liveness.” That is accurate only for a read of integrity-checked historical bytes. A signature proves what a key promised; it does not prove that the header remains on the surviving chain. Signing or submitting a new response transaction is a separate action with current chain and wallet requirements.

## What happens today

The code intentionally treats ordinary provider observation failures differently from integrity failures. Timeouts, reads at different chain points, a moving chain and not-yet-final history retry without creating durable quarantine. A designated integrity error can quarantine the source. See [failure classification](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/l1/source-integrity.ts#L1) and [actual quarantine gate](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L931).

Relevant integrity incidents include a decided header disappearing, changing without explained history, or losing finality. The rollback feed also checks cursor consistency, journal continuity and a rollback crossing persisted decisions. See [decision-transition checks](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.check-l1-rollback-feed.ts#L38) and [rollback checks](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.check-l1-rollback-feed.ts#L103). These conditions justify a safety hold. A new provider response or a restart does not by itself resolve them.

The hold is terminal under the normal store API: `quarantined` cannot become `healthy`, and the next committee tick returns before scanning again. See [terminal state merge](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/store.persisted-decision-transition.ts#L11) and [tick refusal](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L561).

Quarantine also changes every header in the source's persisted-decision set. It marks the header and payload `conflicted`, signatures `post_failed`, and submission/outbox records failed. It does not establish that every affected payload's bytes are actually corrupt. This is a source-authority hold being encoded as an artifact conflict. See JSON quarantine mutation (`demo/da-committee-node/src/store.json-file-committee-store.ts:358`, since deleted); PostgreSQL applies the same policy inside a transaction, [PostgreSQL mutation](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/store/postgres.postgres-committee-store.ts#L399). The JSON-file store was deleted in ticket C2 (#794); the PostgreSQL store is the only one. <!-- doc-links:historical -->

## Why a two-member committee can stay stopped

In a committee of two with a threshold of two, every new attestation needs both members. If either enters the terminal state, the other cannot produce a new quorum. Fixing retries elsewhere cannot supply the missing member's signature. If both share the same rollback or provider incident, both can stop together.

Serving old signatures helps with existing commitments but cannot create signatures for new headers. Consequently, the narrow data-service repair reduces damage to old obligations; proof-based recovery is still needed for new block availability and committee progress.

The word “permanent” needs a qualification: under today's unchanged membership and normal runtime APIs, retries and restarts do not recover. Governance replacement or an explicit safe repair might restore progress. That is outside ordinary self-recovery, and resetting the store to make it appear healthy is not a safe equivalent.

A larger committee with spare members can tolerate some stopped signers, but it does not repair their state. Correlated incidents can still affect everyone. Reducing the threshold solely to hide quarantine changes the committee's trust assumptions; it is not an operational recovery mechanism.

## The bond and accountability consequences

The committee's availability promises are backed by a pooled bond. Applying an attestation binds the header to the signed commitment; an unanswered availability challenge can time out and take a DA bond from that pool. A challenged queue head also stops new appends until closure or timeout. See [bond decision](../../midgard/decisions/da-committee-bond-pool.md#L23) and [response liability](../../../demo/da-committee-node/docs/availability-responder.md#L68).

Thus stopping data service can turn a recoverable observation incident into an actual missed promise. This is a conditional risk: there must be a valid live challenge, no healthy actor must complete the response, and its deadline must pass. Quarantine alone does not immediately slash the pool. This task establishes neither a measured loss nor a probability of loss.

The pool has no per-member accountability: a member that funded nothing may lose nothing directly. A timeout penalizes the shared pool, and a short pool can stop later attestation applications. See [economic consequences](../../midgard/decisions/da-committee-bond-pool.md#L65). Preserve an incident record identifying which source/member stopped, the promises affected, bytes retained, response attempts and recovery evidence. That enables governance and operators to distinguish actual withholding, bad data, an honest safety refusal and implementation failure. It does not authorize automatic slashing outside the protocol's proof rules.

The response is permissionless. A healthy member, watcher or other holder can publish the same committed answer without needing the stopped member to make a new DA signature. The current responder documents this property: [permissionless publication](../../../demo/da-committee-node/docs/availability-responder.md#L60). This is why retaining public access during recovery has value even if the original node cannot safely spend.

## The proposed design

**Keep byte integrity separate from chain authority.** Preserve immutable envelope bytes, their digest, deployment identity, signed availability commitment and signature evidence. Record chain uncertainty separately, with the incident and affected dependencies. A rollback can change whether a header is canonical without changing whether retained bytes match a historical promise. Conversely, bytes that fail their digest check must not become usable merely because chain recovery succeeds.

**Serve by an explicit commitment.** A recovering node may answer a request for the exact historical digest or availability commitment after checking the stored bytes against it. Do not answer an ambiguous “give me the current valid payload for this header” as if canonicality were established. Expose `recovering` or `canonicality_unknown` alongside the historical artifact. The consumer must independently validate the chain when it needs a current protocol decision.

Keep historical signature retrieval distinct from active attestation publication. Returning an immutable signature as evidence is different from announcing that it is currently eligible to apply. Do not make the historical route cause automatic rebroadcast or application of a quarantined decision. A consumer assembling an attestation must check the live header, governed committee and commitment requirements. Existing bytes should remain available for independent examination even if recovery ultimately proves the header orphaned.

**Keep exact-byte checks.** Recompute the envelope digest and verify the frozen availability commitment. The current retained-answer code already performs both checks: [digest and commitment checks](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/availability/retained-payload.ts#L30). Do not replace these with a broad exception for everything marked `conflicted`. The same status can represent divergent bytes, invalid state or source quarantine. Missing or corrupt bytes remain unavailable; alert and attempt bounded recovery from a holder of the exact committed bytes.

**Freeze new decisions while reconstructing evidence.** Recovery must identify a trusted common history point, authenticate the surviving path, explain affected prior observations, and reconcile every dependent attestation, submission and wallet intent. Preserve old signed transactions and signature records. Never clear the no-double-sign memory because a chain point rolled back. The current classifier refuses a different commitment for the same signer/header: [commitment classification](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/peer/signatures.ts#L57), [signing refusal](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/committee-service.committee-service.ts#L749).

Publish recovery atomically, tied to the incident and rollback generation. A stale recovery attempt cannot overwrite a newer incident. Already-broadcast transactions must be reconciled against inclusion, expiry and shared input use before replacements are built. An old signature is never evidence that a transaction which tried to apply it remains included.

**Let independent healthy responders act.** A responder needs an authenticated live challenge with the frozen commitment, verified exact payload bytes, current protocol inputs and a reconciled journal/collateral budget. It does not need the old producer or old signer to be healthy. If the committee's shared source is the disputed dependency, keep that node's mutation held; a separate healthy responder can retrieve the retained answer and complete the obligation. Do not merely replace `assertSourceHealthy` with “the payload was signed before.” The current responder takes successive steps from live challenge state and may publish, settle or close it: [action derivation](../../../demo/da-committee-node/src/availability/responder.ts#L115).

Whether an independently authenticated responder can share one process with the recovering signer is an implementation choice. It needs a real independent authority boundary and appropriate journal/resource isolation; changing a boolean does not create that independence. A5 determines which wallet and transaction dependencies must remain held.

**Recover when proof is sufficient; expose when it is not.** Ordinary transient failures retry with bounded work. A finality incident enters recovery; missing history waits, while irreconcilable authenticated disagreement, wrong deployment identity or corrupted durable signing evidence remains unready and requests intervention. Preserve source generation, recovery cursor, reason and progress across restarts. Do not make timeout or retry exhaustion authorize clearing quarantine, pruning evidence or issuing signatures. B4 provides the fuller recovery classification.

During recovery, keep the read service responsive and show the signing role as unready. Expose separately whether retained promised bytes are available, whether an onchain responder is healthy and whether challenge deadlines are at risk. A single readiness bit cannot explain all three. A5's journal halt can independently block response transactions after committee recovery; advertise that dependency rather than declaring the service fully recovered.

## Alternatives and their tradeoffs

| Alternative | What it gains | What it risks or leaves unresolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preserve today's terminal stop for everything | Simple conservative decision boundary | Disables correct historical data, leaves a two-of-two committee without new quorum and can make old challenges miss deadlines. |
| Clear quarantine after time, retries or restart | Fast apparent resumption | No proof of the correct chain; can repeat signatures or conflicting transactions and hide the original incident. Reject. |
| Allow all `conflicted` payloads to answer challenges | Small apparent fix | Confuses source uncertainty with byte corruption; still fails the separate source/journal guards and can use the wrong bytes. Reject. |
| Serve verified historical bytes, but leave signing quarantine terminal | Protects old obligations and evidence without new committee promises | Does not restore new quorum; independent transaction responder must still be healthy. Good immediate bounded step. |
| Preserve historical service and implement proof-based, dependency-scoped recovery | Restores provably safe decisions while preserving accountability and existing promises | More work: separate statuses, durable recovery publication, source/wallet reconciliation and tests. Recommended target. |

Do not weaken the threshold, recreate the database or automatically refill a slashed pool as substitutes for this work. A repaired process should explain which old facts changed, what promises still exist and why new actions are safe.

## Verification needed before this can be called solved

Test a persisted source incident while retaining a previously signed payload. The exact bytes and historical evidence must remain retrievable, while new signing and attestation application refuse. Wrong deployment, wrong digest, divergent envelope or a corrupted signature must not gain eligibility through the recovery route.

Show a healthy independent responder answering an authenticated canonical challenge using data from the recovering member. Show that an unresolved journal or uncertain live challenge still refuses spending. Include the response deadline; retrieval success alone is not proof that the pool was protected.

Exercise a two-of-two committee through a rollback crossing a persisted decision. Valid recovery must restore enough members to resume new quorum without issuing incompatible signatures. Missing, stale, forged or too-deep recovery evidence must keep decisions stopped. Repeat with crashes at durable publication boundaries and both stores; verify that signature evidence and original transaction bytes survive.

Finally, distinguish an orphaned historical header from a surviving challenged header, and a source quarantine from actual divergent bytes. Test concurrent upload during recovery so A8's immutable binding cannot be overwritten, and show that unrelated work proceeds only when shared source/wallet dependencies are sound. These are acceptance requirements, not tests performed for this report.
