## 0. Protocol invariants and scope

Six invariants (`C-considerations/1-protocol-invariants.tex:10-67`): block merging order,
**valid blocks always mergeable (soundness)**, **invalid blocks always removable
(completeness — the subject of this matrix)**, operator rotation liveness, operator bond
locked until maturity, censorship protection via gapless event intervals. There is **no
DA-availability invariant** in that file; DA criticality is asserted at
`technical-spec/6-offchain-data-architecture/1-da-layer.tex:8` and the withheld-data risk
admitted at `:117-118`.

Scope of this matrix: every transaction rule and state-transition invariant an operator
could violate in a committed block, plus the data-availability and state-queue-correction
machinery the proofs depend on. Mempool admission (Phase A/B) is _defense_, not
_enforcement_ — "reject codes and details are operational evidence, not L1 fault proofs"
(`technical-spec/7-phase-two-validation/3-fraud-proofs-involved.tex:9`) — so a row is only
**Complete** when the L1 path works.

note only family-specific correction behavior.

## 11. Cross-cutting disagreements (spec ↔ on-chain ↔ off-chain ↔ ops ↔ tests)

1. **Completeness invariant vs implementation** — spec claims a verifier per violation
   (`1-protocol-invariants.tex:33`); §§4, 5, 7, 9 show whole families missing.
2. **Catalogue: 29 positional categories (`00000000`–`0000001c`).** Generic
   `submit-init`, runtime deployment resolution, inspection, and node/core
   manifest schemas share the order.
   `transitionTrace` remains `00000004` with one route plus eight finals. Every
   registered step is a mandatory authenticated reference script, and the
   identity change is fresh-genesis/redeploy only (no migration/compatibility).
   This closes catalogue/deployment registration, not family-specific CLI,
   autonomous watcher detection/proving, or live acceptance.
3. **Some locally enforced rules still lack a complete standalone L1 path** —
   min-fee, value preservation, and native-policy mint authorization now have
   registered standalone families; min-ada and Plutus execution are
   machine-disputable; network-id is registered and locally proven; missing
   UTxO native-script material and native-script-invalid still lack their
   required standalone families.
4. **Local reject taxonomy is disjoint from proof categories by design** — admission
   handles pre-block rejected transactions, but no detector maps a committed block's
   violation to a proof family either (watcher gap).
5. **Q54 retention enforcement is complete** (§9): derived retention is bound through
   the manifest, node/committee configuration, pruning/readiness, and alerts. The
   deliberately inert committee-store pruner is a Q58/W-O7 residual, not a Q54 failure.
6. **Spec self-inconsistency**: WITHDRAWN-REFERENCE-INPUT defined (`:886`) but missing
   from its rule's violation list (`:837-841`).
7. **Descendant correction release gap** — structural pruning,
   rotated-operator unit evidence, and transactional node-side re-inclusion
   exist; real-node concurrent and preprod correction acceptance do not.
8. **Naming**: public documentation uses `fault proof`, while several source
   paths still use `fraud-proof/` and `fraud-proofs/` names (W-O8).
9. **Preprod evidence**: no current, reproducible preprod
   proof-through-removal artifact is published for the current blueprint,
   catalogue, deployment manifest, and reference-script identities (W-T4).
10. **Witness-set encoding is reconciled by Q00**: native V1 witness verification is
    shared by the signature families. Their remaining Q15/Q16 status is atomic closure,
    not a `Pairs`-versus-native-list binding split.
11. **On-chain proof decoders are looser than the off-chain canonical codec**: the
    on-chain value decoder accepts duplicate and unsorted policy ids/asset names and
    empty asset names (`native-tx/components.ak:396-450`); required-signer/observer
    entries are not length-checked (`native-tx/preimages.ak:94-108`); the full-body
    decode path skips the `network_id` range check and the 32-byte checks on the two
    integrity hashes that the compact path enforces (`native-tx/transaction.ak:280-292`);
    the mint preimage decodes as raw `Data` with structure enforced only on encode
    (`native-tx/preimages.ak:292-294`). Consequences cut both ways: value-sum proofs
    (W-C2) built on a decoder that tolerates duplicate asset keys can double-count
    (soundness), and preimages only the loose decoder accepts may defeat honest tooling
    (completeness). Needs a stated canonical-equivalence requirement + property tests
    (W-T2 widened to on-chain decoders).

## 11b. Cross-cutting completeness requirement: provability under adversarial sizing

A proof family only satisfies the completeness invariant if its **worst-case evidence
physically fits** Cardano's transaction-size and execution-unit limits — otherwise an
operator can make a violation unprovable by inflating the offending transaction (a
provability-DoS), even though the rule and verifier exist. Canonical V1 therefore
uses independently authenticated field preimages:

- Only double-spend has an explicit mitigation: the spend-input reference-witness UTxO
  (`demo/midgard-fault-proofs/src/spend-input-witness.ts`), emulator-tested at 180 inputs
  (`tests/spend-input-witness.test.ts:32-68`).
- There is no independent 8 KiB canonical V1 transaction ceiling. Each dynamic
  field has a compiled maximum derived from the supported 16 KiB L1 floor and
  that instruction's concrete proof overhead. The effective canonical
  transaction maximum is the sum of all wrapped dynamic-field maxima plus
  fixed fields.
- Ledger-output preimages are bounded by their membership-proof envelope.
  Values and datums have no arbitrary 1 KiB sub-cap. Reference scripts are
  supported within the containing output and script-program envelopes.
- Forced-order fields use mandatory staged receipt NFTs. A fragment is first
  published, then independently referenced and verified by the receipt policy.
  The final order verifies nine compact receipts rather than aggregating all
  field preimages. The measured maxima are 9,746 bytes for an unsigned field
  publication, 11,408,043 memory / 3,497,630,466 CPU for one receipt proof,
  and 3,204,296 / 1,246,065,449 for final receipt verification.
- Novel forced-transaction CEK graphs are published permissionlessly as
  immutable, content-addressed L1 datum outputs before the order. The order
  commits only the program roots/counts, so there is no unbounded reference
  list. A maximum node publication datum is 4,268 bytes and its measured
  one-node unsigned transaction is 4,369 bytes; missing graph nodes block
  classification/building instead of becoming an operator-selected
  `FailedScript` verdict.
- Per-source counts are bounded to 10,000; total events, trace steps, and ledger
  operations to 40,000; canonical transaction bytes to 16 MiB; and the
  DA payload to 64 MiB. L1 state-queue validation enforces header versions and
  counts. The DA committee enforces canonical payload/transaction bytes and
  features before signing, and merge requires its attestation.
- The node planner returns an empty batch when the first candidate does not fit;
  it no longer admits one oversized candidate as a convenience.

Status: 🔶 D-S1 is resolved for canonical V1. The derived bounds are enforced
in the shared profile, but release remains blocked until the V1 field-preimage
machine and validator-hash-bound worst-case sizing evidence cover every
instruction. Every family's acceptance criteria in
[`execution-plan.md`](execution-plan.md) §10 still requires a concrete
worst-case sizing and execution-unit argument (W-T8).

## 12. Severity ranking of open gaps (adversarial view)

Fund-theft-capable, in rough order of exploitability:

1. **Native-script-invalid and missing UTxO script material** (§5) — spend
   other people's funds. Value conservation, native-policy mint authorization,
   input uniqueness, spend-side withdrawn-input, fabricated-event, and
   cross-block duplicate-event families are registered; their residual risk is
   operational actuation/live evidence rather than missing L1 logic.
2. **DA withholding after attestation** (§9) — make any of the above unprovable.
3. **Adversarial-sizing provability DoS, per-tx and per-block** (§11b) — make any of the
   above unprovable by inflating the offending transaction or block.
4. **State-correction integration gaps** (§10) — structural descendant
   correction and local transaction/event re-inclusion are implemented, but
   real-node concurrency and preprod acceptance are not proven.
5. **Zeroed economics** — removes deterrence and realized prover reward even where
   proofs work. Canonical seven-day maturity identity is resolved.

Everything else (tooling absence, autonomous watcher gaps, live acceptance, and test
debt) is operational readiness rather than protocol soundness. Catalogue registration
alone does not ensure a fault is detected or a proof is submitted in time.

The independent **escape hatch** and worst-case proof completion inside the
seven-day maturity window are the leading liveness/completeness risks. Q61 now
